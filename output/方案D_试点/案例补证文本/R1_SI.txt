Experiment 
Preparation of carbon/sulfur (CS) composite: CS composites were prepared by the 
simple high-energy ball milling process. Acetylene black (AB) and sublimed sulfur 
were weighted in the ratio of 1:2, then mixed by high-energy ball milling (Pulverisette7, 
Fritsch) at a rotating speed of 500 rpm for 12 h in a stainless-steel ZrO2 jar. 
Preparation of LPO coating layer: LPO films were deposited on the surface of CS 
particles in a Yunmao GM10 powder ALD system. Lithium tert-butoxide (LiOtBu), 
Trimethyl phosphate (TMP) and H2O were used as the precursors. Before deposition, 
0.3g CS powder as the reaction substrate was uniformly spread onto stainless steel 
screen in the ALD reactor. Due to the low sublimation temperature of elemental sulfur, 
the deposition temperature is kept at 90°C. LiOtBu and TMPO precursors were 
introduced into the reactor alternately with a pulse time of 2 s, and 20 sccm of Ar was 
used as the carrier gas to purge for 30 s after each pulse. The rotation speed of the 

=== 第 3 页 ===
reactor is 30 rpm/min to ensure uniform coating on the surface of CS particles. After 
12 and 24 ALD cycles, the LPO coating with different thickness was deposited on the 
surface of CS powder. The same processes were performed on AB particles to obtained 
LPO@AB materials. Then, the obtained LPO@AB and sulfur powder were 
mechanically ball milled at a weight ratio of 1:2 to prepare LPO@AB-S composite 
material. 
Preparation of composite cathode: The synthesized AB/S, LPO@AB-S and 
LPO@AB/S powders were mixed with LPS solid electrolyte at a weight ratio of 3:2, 
respectively. The composite cathodes were obtained by ball milling at 400 rpm for 9 h 
in a stainless-steel jar. To prepare AB-LPS and LPO@AB-LPS, the LPS electrolyte was 
mixed with AB and LPO@AB in a weight ratio of 2:1, respectively, in the same 
procedure. 
Physical characterization: X-ray diffraction (XRD) data was collected on X-ray 
diffractometer (Rigaku Ultima IV) using Cu Kα radiation (λ=1.5471 Å) over the range 
of 5-80° with 5°/min scanning speed. The surface morphology of composites was 
acquired at scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (Hitachi S-4800) equipped with 
energy dispersive spectroscopy (EDS). Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) 
studied was conducted using FEI-F20 instrument. The sulfur content of different carbon 
composites was obtained by thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) (TG, SQT Q600) 
under N2 flow at a heating rate of 10 °C/min. Raman spectra was recorded by the 
confocal microscope Raman system Xplora (Horiba Jobin Yvon) with a laser 
wavelength of 532 nm. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were 
performed on Thermo Scientific Escalab 250Xi+, calibrated with the C 1s peak of 
adventitious carbon at 284.8 eV. Time of Flight Secondary Ion Mass Spectrometry 
(ToF-SIMS) measurements were conducted using a ToF-SIMS instrument (IONTOF 
M6) equipped with a 30keV Bi cluster primary ion gun for analysis and a 1keV Cs+ ion 
beam for depth profiling. Sulfur K-edge X-ray absorption near edge structure (XANES) 
spectra were collected at the 4B7A beamline of the Beijing Synchrotron Radiation 
Facility (BSRF). 
Fabrication of ASSLSBs: The ASSLSBs with Li–In|LPSCl|S configuration were 
prepared in customized solid-state battery molds. The solid-state electrolyte layer was 
prepared by pressing 100 mg of LPSCl into a polytetrafluoroethylene (PEEK) sleeve 
(diameter of 10 mm). Then 3-17.6 mg of the cathode composite materials such as AB/S, 

=== 第 4 页 ===
LPO@AB-S and LPO@AB/S were evenly dispersed and pressed on the one side of the 
electrolyte layer with a weight of 6 tons. Finally, a piece of Li0.6In alloy foil was 
attached to the other side of the SSE layer and pressed at 0.5 tons. The pressure of 
ASSLSBs was fixed at around 60 MPa by tightening the frame. All processes were 
performed in a glove box with H2O and O2 < 0.1 ppm. 
Ionic and electronic conductivity test: The DC polarization techniques were applied to 
evaluate the ionic and electronic conductivity of composites. The ion-blocking cells 
were prepared for electronic conductivity testing. 50 mg of composite powder (AB/S, 
LPO@AB-S or LPO@AB/S) was loaded into a mold cell with a Ø10 mm PEEK sleeve 
and compressed at 400 MPa. The ionic conductivities of composite cathodes were 
measured with an electron-blocking symmetric cell. First, 50 mg of cathode composite 
material was poured into a mold and compressed at 200 MPa. Then, 100 mg of LPSCl 
electrolyte was pressed onto each side of the cathode composite layer at 400 MPa. 
Finally, Li-In anode serving as Li reservoir was attached to both sides by a pressure of 
100 MPa. Both blocking cells were compressed at the pressure of 60 MPa during testing. 
After resting for 1 h under open circuit conditions, the constant potentials of -40, -20, -
10, 0, +10, +20, +40 mV were applied and maintained for 1 h to ensure the equilibrium 
of cell polarization. The partial resistances were calculated using Ohm’s law (R = U/I). 
The conductivity can be expressed by the following formula: σ = (R × A) / l (where R, 
A, and l represent resistance, area, and thickness, respectively). 
Electrochemical measurement: The galvanostatic discharge/charge tests of ASSLSBs 
were performed using a battery test system (Neware) with an environmental chamber 
at temperature of 30°C and 60℃ in a voltage range of 0.9-2.4 V (vs. Li-In) or 1.5-3.0 
V (vs. Li/Li+). Cyclic voltammetry (CV) experiments were investigated by a Versa 
STATV Multichannel potentiostat/galvanostat at a potential range of 0.9-2.4 V (vs. Li-
In). Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) tests were carried out from 0.1Hz 
to 1 MHz with an amplitude of 10 mV at 30℃ by a Versa STATV Multichannel 
Potentiostat/Galvanostat. 
Stack pressure measurement: The in-situ pressure monitoring of ASSLSBs with 
different cathodes was conducted with a homemade apparatus. The force sensor was 
installed at the bottom of the battery mold. This allows to monitor the uniaxial stress 
response during discharge/charge process. The initial stack pressure of the battery 
assembly was controlled by tightening the bolt on the top metal plate. The stack
