Unraveling the reversible redox mechanism of
Li6PS5Cl solid electrolyte in all-solid-state
lithium–sulfur batteries†

We reveal the reversible redox mechanism of Li6PS5Cl in composite
cathodes for practical all-solid-state lithium–sulfur batteries. The lim-
ited electrochemical reactivity of the non-conducting sulfur-based
active material induces the formation of Li6PS5Cl decomposition pro-
ducts, which allows the recrystallization of Li6PS5Cl through a reversible
redox process, contributing to the composite cathode capacity.
Safety issues surrounding lithium-ion secondary batteries (LIBs)
have urged the development of ion-conductive solid electrolytes
(SEs) to prevent thermal runaway by replacing the flammable liquid
electrolyte (LE) with non-flammable SEs.1–6 The ceramic SEs for all-
solid-state batteries (ASSBs) are also expected to provide a higher
energy density limit compared to LIBs by enabling the use of a
lithium (Li) metal anode solving the safety issues at once.2,7–11
Specifically, in conventional LIBs employing LEs, Li metal dendrite
growth from the anode penetrates the separator toward the cathode
side, causing short circuits and eventually thermal runaway.10,11 In
this regard, the mechanical strength of ceramic SEs can prevent the
Li dendrite penetration through the SE, resulting in safe high
energy density ASSBs.5,6,9–11 However, when replacing the LE with a
SE, issues such as poor ionic conductivity, narrow electrochemical
stability window, and irreversible decomposition at the electrode
interfacial layer resulting in increased interfacial resistance, have
hindered their use in alternating LIBs.1,2,5,9–11 Among various ASSB
systems, all-solid-state lithium–sulfur batteries (ASSLSBs) consist-
ing of Li-based alloy anodes and sulfur-based cathodes with sulfide
SEs have attracted huge attention as one of the promising next-
generation ultra-high energy density secondary batteries.5,10 In
particular, Li6PS5Cl (LPSCl) has emerged as a promising SE mate-
rial for ASSLSBs that can compensate for the aforementioned
drawbacks of SEs since it exhibits high ionic conductivity and
superior formability to other types of SEs.3–5,10,11 Meanwhile,
several studies have proposed new future development directions
for ASSLSBs that consider LPSCl as a redox active material in the
cathode and investigate its redox reaction mechanism by fabricat-
ing a single LPSCl cathode system (solely consisting of SE).1–3,9,12
However, such a single SE cathode system which was artificially
created to investigate the redox reaction of the SE material certainly
excludes the interface interactions between the cathode/anode
active materials and the SE particles in a practical composite
cathode.1 Therefore, the redox reaction of LPSCl should be system-
atically investigated in the composite cathode in ASSLSBs to attain
insights to develop practical high-energy density ASSBs. To our
knowledge, only a few studies have investigated the redox reaction
of LPSCl in the composite cathode to which NCM-based cathode
active materials are applied.3,6 In particular, the research on the
reaction of LPSCl in the composite cathode, where sulfur-based
cathode active materials exist, is still insufficient.
In this study, we explore the redox reaction of LPSCl in the
composite cathode of ASSLSB consisting of sulfur-based cathode
active materials and LPSCl rather than a single SE cathode system.
In our experimental conditions, the sulfur-based cathode active
material with insulating properties10 are added to the composite
cathode, creating an environment where electrochemical reactivity
is limited compared to a single SE cathode system. The practical
redox reaction of LPSCl in an actual battery configuration is
examined by X-ray analyses. According to previous literature based
on a single SE cathode system, it is found that LPSCl decomposes
into LiCl and LixP (0 r x r 3) during discharge and produces
oxidized products such as S and P2S5 during charge.2,3,9,12 Whereas,
in our work, a diﬀerence is discovered only during charge. The
comprehensive analysis results imply that LPSCl is decomposed as
mentioned above during discharge, but reversibly returns to LPSCl

For the experiment, the crystal structure of the synthesized
LPSCl (details in the ESI†) is confirmed through the X-ray diﬀrac-

redox reaction of SE, LPSCl is mixed with multi-walled carbon
nanotubes (MWCNTs) to form a composite cathode (LPSCl-C)
(details in the ESI†), and then charge/discharge cycling tests are
conducted on the LPSCl–C/LPSCl/Li–In cell. Fig. 1c indicates the
stable cycling performance of the cell with 100% coulombic
efficiency after formation cycling. Besides, a reversible discharge
capacity of B50 mA h g1 is observed for over 500 cycles (Fig. 1d).

Furthermore, this description is supported by the cell impedance
spectra after initial charge/discharge (Fig. S1, ESI†) and cyclic
voltammogram (CV) curves during initial cycling (Fig. S2, ESI†).
The reduced/oxidized products after initial cycling do not contri-
bute to a significant change in the interfacial resistance since the
cell impedance is measured in a similar resistance range. More-
over, considering that the cell impedance after discharge is eval-
uated lower than after charge, it is inferred that the decomposition
products generated reversibly during discharge play a beneficial
role in the interfacial resistance. Even in the CV curves, no
formation/extinction or shifts of new redox peaks are found until
the initial third cycle. Consequently, the decomposition products of
LPSCl contribute to maintaining the cell impedance, allowing the
reversible capacity to last for 500 cycles.
To determine the impact of LPSCl on the ASSLSBs, the cycling
performances of ASSLSBs employing various types of SEs are com-
pared in Fig. 2. The sulfur-based composite cathode (S–C) is
composed of sulfur-based active material, MWCNTs and SE (details
in the ESI†). The S–C/LPSCl/Li–In cell reveals that a small amount of
capacity is reversibly exhibited even after the 250th cycle and shows a
high initial discharge specific capacity of 1193 mA h g1. In contrast,
the S–C/Li3PS4 (LPS)/Li–In cell and S–C/Li3YCl6 (LYCl)/Li–In cell
demonstrate that the capacity converges to zero even though the
same cathode/anode active materials are used. In addition, only 811
and 836 mA h g1 are detected as the initial discharge specific
capacities of the cells with LPS and LYCl, respectively. To properly
operate the ultra-high energy density ASSLSBs, SEs should meet
several criteria, such as high ionic conductivity, deformability,
and electrochemical stability windows suitable for sulfur-based
cathodes.4,11,13 Thus, the comparison results based on these criteria
show that LPSCl is the most desirable material for ASSLSBs. Since the
ionic conductivity of LPSCl is higher than those of LPS and LYCl (Fig.
S3, ESI†), it is sufficient to utilize insulating sulfur-based cathode
active materials. Besides, the reversible capacity of LPSCl contributes
to the capacity of the ASSLSB for long-term cycling (Fig. 1c and d),
unlike the other two types of SEs. X-ray analyses are performed to
investigate the mechanism by which redox reaction of LPSCl con-
tributes to the reversibility of S–C/LPSCl/Li–In the cell. First of all, to
verify reduced/oxidized products generated on the surface of the SE
particles, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis is carried
out by focusing on the Cl and P elements contained in LPSCl, as
shown in Fig. 3a and b. The binding energies of the elements in the
composite cathode after charge/discharge are compared to those of
pristine materials LPSCl, LiCl and P2S5. The Cl 2p binding energy of
LPSCl is detected at 198.5 eV and the doublet peaks are maintained
until the 5th cycle, but additional Cl 2p peaks of LiCl occur at
197.5 eV only after discharge (Fig. 3a). This tendency is similarly
confirmed in the XPS measurement results of the P element. The
P 2p binding energy of LPSCl is observed at 131.6 eV and the doublet
peaks remain during cycling, but changes in shape appear only in the
spectra after discharge (Fig. 3b). However, in contrast to the genera-
tion of LiCl as a single reduced product associated with the Cl
element, the formation of various reduced products related to
P element, such as LixP (0 r x r 3), is predicted by the occurrence
of the spectral shape changes in irregular and extensive regions
below 130 eV. Besides, the new peaks in regions above 130 eV are not

To identify this mechanism more specifically, X-ray absorption
near edge spectroscopy (XANES) analysis of Cl and P elements is
performed at diﬀerent voltage sections during initial cycling, as
shown in Fig. 4. The XANES spectra of the composite cathodes
under diﬀerent degrees of lithiation are compared to those of
pristine materials LPSCl, LiCl and P2S5. In the case of Cl element,
the K-edge of LiCl is detected in a lower energy region than LPSCl,
and the K-edges of LPSCl and the initial composite cathode are
observed in the same energy region (Fig. 4a). However, there is a
diﬀerence between the spectra of the composite cathodes during
cycling (Fig. 4b). The spectra of 0.4 V and 0.1 V in the lithiation
process shift to the lower energy region compared to the initial
state as indicated by a red arrow since the LiCl is generated as one
of the reduced products during discharge. LiCl is converted back to
LPSCl during charge, so the spectrum of the fully charged state at
3.0 V moves to the higher energy region than that of the fully
discharged state at 0.1 V as indicated by a blue arrow. This shift,
depending on the state of charge and discharge, can be supported
more clearly in the Cl L-edge spectra (Fig. 4c). The spectra of LPSCl
and the initial composite cathode have similar shape and energy
region, but the spectra in the lithiation process have diﬀerent
shapes and are detected in the low energy region. After that, the
spectrum of the fully charged state at 3.0 V shifts to the higher
energy region and is restored to a shape similar to that of LPSCl. It
is remarkable that the LiCl formation reaction is already completed
above 0.4 V in the lithiation process, since there is no significant
change in the spectral shape between 0.4 V and 0.1 V. Even for the

This study explores the redox reaction of LPSCl in the
practical
composite
cathode
environment
where
electro-
chemical reactivity is limited by using sulfur-based cathode
active materials. The electrochemistry and X-ray based analysis
results demonstrate the reversible redox reaction of LPSCl in
the ASSLSB that we report for the first time. The LPSCl particle
interface is decomposed into reduced products during dis-
charge, e.g., LiCl and LixP (0 r x r 3), but returns to the
initial LPSCl without oxidized products during charge. Further-
more, the reduction products provide beneficial eﬀects on the
long-term cycling performance of ASSLSB by providing stable
surface resistance and improving the reversibility of the
ASSLSB. Moreover, it is confirmed that the oxidation stability
of LPSCl is also enhanced due to the limited electrochemical
reactivity of the non-conductive sulfur-based cathode active
material. Our study further suggests that the electrochemical
redox mechanism of the materials established from the artifi-
cial environment can diﬀer from the one found in practical
ASSLSBs.
