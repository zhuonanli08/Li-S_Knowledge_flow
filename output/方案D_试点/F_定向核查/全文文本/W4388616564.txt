Active Regulation Volume Change of Micrometer-Size Li2S
Cathode with High Materials Utilization for All-Solid-State
Li/S Batteries through an Interfacial Redox Mediator
Peiwen Yu, Shaorui Sun, Chunhao Sun, Chaoyuan Zeng, Ze Hua, Niaz Ahmad,*
Ruiwen Shao,* and Wen Yang*
Low electronic and ionic transport, limited cathode active material utilization,
and signiﬁcant volume change have pledged the practical application of
all-solid-state Li/S batteries (ASSLSBs). Herein, an unprecedented
Li2S-LixIn2S3 cathode is designed whereby In2S3 reacts with Li2S under
high-energy ball milling. In situ electron diﬀraction and ex situ XPS are
implanted to probe the reaction mechanism of Li2S-LixIn2S3 in ASSLSBs. The
results indicate that LixIn2S3 serves as a mobility mediator for both
charge-carriers (Li+ and e−) and redox mediator for Li2S activation, ensuring
eﬃcient electronic and ionic transportation at the cathode interface and
inhibiting ≈70% relative volumetric change in the cathode, as conﬁrmed by
in situ TEM. Thus, the Li2S-LixIn2S3 cathode delivers an initial areal capacity
of 3.47 mAh cm−2 at 4.0 mgLi2S cm−2 with 78% utilization of Li2S. A
solid-state cell with Li2S-LixIn2S3 cathode carries 82.35% capacity retention
over 200 cycles at 0.192 mA cm−2 and a remarkable rate capability up to
0.64 mA cm−2 at RT. Besides, Li2S-LixIn2S3 exhibits the highest initial areal
capacity of 4.08 mAh cm−2 with ≈74.01% capacity retention over 50 cycles
versus 6.6 mgLi2S cm−2 at 0.192 mA cm−2 at RT. The proposed strategy of the
redox mediator minimized volumetric change and realized outstanding
electrochemical performance for ASSLSBs.

Especially sulﬁde outstanding ion conductors, such as Li6PS5Cl,
Li10GeP2S12, and Li7P3S11, arise as the most promising solid-
state electrolytes (SSEs) for ASSLSBs due to their high ionic
conductivity and excellent mechanical deformability.[12–14] How-
ever, the practical applications of ASSLSBs are still hampered
due to the insulator nature of sulfur (ca. electric conductiv-
ity of 5 × 10−30 S cm−1 at 25°C) and its discharge product
Li2S (ca. electric conductivity of 10−13 S cm−1 at 25 °C) for
both electric and ionic conduction.[15,16] The limited contact
area on the triple-phase boundary (active materials, conduc-
tivity agent and SSEs) further worsens the electrochemical
performance of ASSLSBs.[17] Therefore, ASSLSBs exhibit low
sulfur utilization and poor cycle life at room temperature,
and high sulfur utilization of ASSLSBs can only be received
under high temperatures, such as 60 °C.[17,18] Constructing
inherent electron and ionic conduction paths at the cathode
triple-phase interface is essential to achieve high-performance
ASSLSBs.[19]
Another
issue hampering the practical applications
of
ASSLSBs is the colossal volume change of the active materials.[20]
A substantial volume change (≈80%) can be observed under the
electrochemical reaction: S8 + 16Li →8Li2S.[15,21,22] Li2S has a
high theoretical capacity of 1166 mAh g−1, considered one of
the most promising cathode in ASSLSBs.[23–25] However, the
research on volume change in micrometer-size Li2S cathode in
ASSLSBs is always ignored. The partial molar volume of Li2S
is 2.768 × 10−5 m3 mol−1, which is twice as large as the sulfur,
indicating that the high volume shrank during the de-lithiation
process of Li2S.[26]
Furthermore, compressive and tensile stress is produced at
the cathode interface when converting 8 moles Li2S to 1 mol S8,
and mechanical deformation has been observed on the pouch
cell.[27–30] This mechanical deformation at the cathode interface
is induced by volume shrink or expansion, which can worsen un-
der high cathode loading.[31,32] It may also deteriorate the inter-
face contact between the active material and SSEs in ASSLSBs,
thus leading to poor active material utilization and low cycle life
of ASSLSBs.
Herein, we propose a facile interfacial redox mediator strat-
egy to boost the kinetic behavior of the Li2S cathode and,
more importantly, to regulate the volume change of the cath-
ode in ASSLSBs actively and, thus, the superior electrochem-
ical performance of ASSLSBs is achieved with high cathode
loading. We designed an unprecedented Li2S-LixIn2S3 compos-
ite cathode structure by In2S3 reacting with Li2S cathode un-
der high-energy ball milling. The resultant Li2S-LixIn2S3 cath-
ode realizes superior electrochemical performance, for exam-
ple, the initial areal capacity of 3.47 mAh cm−2 at 4.0 mgLi2S
cm−2 with 78% utilization of Li2S. In addition, the solid-
state cell with Li2S-LixIn2S3 cathode exhibits a remarkable rate
capability up to 0.64 mA cm−2. Furthermore, the cell pre-
sented excellent capacity retention of 82.35% over 200 cycles
at 0.192 mA cm−2. Besides, Li2S-LixIn2S3 delivered the highest
initial areal capacity of 4.08 mAh cm−2 and retained ≈74.01%
capacity retention over 50 cycles versus 6.6 mgLi2S cm−2 at
0.192 mA cm−2 at RT, which could be attributed the redox
mediator In2S3 and LixIn2S3 reported for the ﬁrst time in
ASSLSBs.
2. Experimental Section
2.1. Preparation of Active Material
Li2S-LixIn2S3 were produced by a simple ball milling with a
mixture of Li2S and In2S3. Li2S (Alfa, 99.9%) was mixed with
xIn2S3 (Macklin, 99%) in varying proportions (x = 0.033, 0.067,
0.1), and the sample with the ratio of 0.1 is deﬁned as Li2S-
0.1In. The mixture was transferred to 45 ml zirconia tanks
ﬁlled with ﬁve zirconia balls (diameter is 5 mm) and milled
for 10 h at 370 rpm. The heat of the solid-state reaction was
released by suspending the milling operation for 15 min per
cycle.
2.2. Preparation of Electrolyte Li7P3S11
The electrolyte was synthesized according to the previous re-
search reports.[11] The Li2S (Alfa, 99.9%) and P2S5 (Macklin,
99.99%) with a mole ratio of 7:3 as the raw materials were mixed
and ball milled at 510 rpm for 45 h, followed by heating at 280 °C
for 2 h to get the glass-ceramics Li7P3S11 SSE.
2.3. Preparation of Cathode Composition
A two-step ball-milling process synthesized the Li2S-LixIn2S3
composite cathode. First step: Li2S-LixIn2S3 and VGCF powders
were mixed in a 3:1 (wt.%) ratio and milled for 9 h at 370 rpm.
Second step: The Li2S-LixIn2S3-VGCF composite and Li7P3S11
SSE were incorporated in a 4:6 (wt.%) and ball milled for 1 h
at 150 rpm just for homogeneous mixing. The exact process was
repeated for the preparation of the Li2S cathode.
2.4. Fabricating ASSLSBs
ASSLSBs were assembled with Li7P3S11 SSE. First, 150 mg
Li7P3S11 electrolyte powder was put in the polyvinylidene diﬂuo-
ride (PVDF) model and cold pressed at 360 MPa. Second, a cer-
tain weight of Li2S-LixIn2S3 cathode powder was distributed ho-
mogenously on the top of the solid-electrolyte pellet and pressed
under 360 MPa. Thirdly, the Li-In alloy anode (a piece of lithium
foil with a thickness of 30 μm and a bit of indium foil with a thick-
ness of 100 μm in a weight ratio of 5:95 were pushed together
under appropriate pressure. Lithium metal diﬀuses readily into
the indium matrix. It can be converted into a Li-In alloy thanks
to the metals’ advantageous mechanical characteristics). It was
placed on the opposite side and pressed again at 120 MPa. Fi-
nally, the triple-layered pellet was sandwiched between stainless
steel current collectors.

X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) structural characterization was recorded
using the Bruker D8 instrument with Cu K𝛼radiation from 20° to
70°. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) measurements were

and discharge states. The battery was taken apart when the bat-

remove the cathode layer from the mold carefully and prepare
for follow-up testing. As for in situ TEM, Talos F200X at 200 kV
(Thermo Fisher Scientiﬁc Company) was used to collect the ex-
perimental data of volume change and selected area electron
diﬀraction (SAED) patterns of the sample during de-lithiation
and lithiation. Moreover, the microscope image was not cor-
rected, and the high tension was 200 KV. The camera used in the
study is Ceta, the magniﬁcation is 22 500×, and the image size is
1024 × 1024. Li2S or Li2S-LixIn2S3 samples were mounted onto a
half-copper grid as the working electrode, and lithium metal (act-
ing as the counter electrode) was attached to a tungsten probe.
During the transfer of the TEM specimen holder into the TEM
chamber, the lithium metal was exposed to air (< 10 s) to form
a layer of LixO that served as the solid electrolyte, which allowed
the transport of Li ions. The LixO/Li electrode approached the
Li2S or Li2S-LixIn2S3 electrode by driving the mobile probe care-
fully. After establishing contact, a bias of +3.0 V was applied to
extract Li+ from the pristine sample. Then, the bias was reversed,
and a voltage of −3.0 V was applied for lithiation. All samples
were sensitive to the atmospheric environment, and all proce-
dures were conducted in the Ar-ﬁlled glovebox (O2 ≤0.1 ppm,

3. Results and Discussion
The phase identiﬁcation of newly developed Li2S-LixIn2S3 was in-
vestigated via Powder XRD. As shown in Figure 1b, all the sam-
ples have a basic structure similar to cubic Li2S. However, it is
noticed that a faint and relatively broad peak at 2𝜃= 33.40° could
be revealed to the LixIn2S3 in the Li2S matrix. The LixIn2S3 is pro-
duced by reducing In2S3 by Li2S,[15,33] its structure is similar to
the primal In2S3.
[34,35] Transmission electron microscopic (TEM)
measurements were conducted for Li2S-0.1In, as illustrated in
Figure 1c,d. Figure 1c shows a typical TEM bright-ﬁeld image of
the Li2S-0.1In material, and the corresponding selected area elec-
tron diﬀraction (SAED) image (Figure 1d) shows a polycrystalline
structure. As shown in Figure 1d, there are four diﬀraction rings
whereby yellow representing the (220) and (200) planes of Li2S,
and light blue diﬀraction ring representing the (419) and (109)
planes of LixIn2S3 along with (331) and (111) stems from Li2S in
the SAED pattern, which is consistent with the XRD results.
Besides, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) of Li2S-0.1In
was performed to gain insight into the chemical states of the el-
ements. In S 2p spectra (Figure S1a, Supporting Information),
the doublet peak at 160.2 and 161.3 eV indexed to S-Li and In-
S from Li2S and LixIn2S3, respectively.[36,37] In addition, In 3d
(Figure S1b, Supporting Information), two sharp peaks at
444.1 and 451.7 eV are ascribed to In 3d5/2 and 3d3/2,
respectively.[36,38,39] Noteworthy, the binding energies of Li2S-
0.1In slightly shift to lower compared to pure In2S3, indicating
the increase in the electronic density of In3+ after the reaction
with Li2S.[40] Furthermore, X-ray absorption near-edge structure
(XANES) was also applied to disclose the local structural en-
vironment of Li2S-0.1In. The S K-edge (Figure S2, Supporting
Information) proﬁle of the Li2S-0.1In is analogous to pristine
Li2S with an apparent pre-edge peak ≈2472.5 eV.[41,42] Besides,
two small shoulder peaks at 2471 and 2475 eV are associated
with LixIn2S3, consistent with PXRD and XPS. The microscale
elemental distributions of the Li2S-LixIn2S3 cathode were eval-
uated using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and energy-
dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) elemental mapping, as shown in
Figure 1e. The elemental mapping results indicate a uniform dis-
tribution of sulfur and Indium throughout the cathode. Naturally,
insulating characteristics of Li2S (𝜎Li+:10−11 S cm and 𝜎e−:10−13 S
cm−1) cause high electrode polarization, and slow reaction kinet-
ics is a principal culprit of substandard performance.[43–45] The
𝜎e−of the Li2S-LixIn2S3 was determined from direct-current po-
larization curves at 2 V at RT,[46,47] as illustrated in Figure S3 (Sup-
porting Information). All the samples have better 𝜎e−compared
to pristine Li2S. Mainly, 𝜎e−of Li2S-0.1In was determined to be
1.2 × 10−8 S cm−1, ≈5 orders of magnitude higher than pristine
Li2S (10−13 S cm−1).[17,48] Next, the 𝜎Li+ of Li2S-0.1In was deter-
mined to be 3.3 × 10−9 S cm−1, two order of magnitude higher
than the pristine Li2S. The correlation of 𝜎Li+ and activation en-
ergy (Ea) is further elaborated in Figure S4 (Supporting Informa-
tion).

Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) explored the
interfacial resistance before and after cycling. Before cycling, the
solid-state cells Li2S-0.1In//Li-In and Li2S//Li-In with Li7P3S11
SE present 50.5 and 62.8 Ω impedance, respectively (Figure 2c).
However, on the other hand, over the battery testing, the
impedance of Li2S-0.1In//Li-In and Li2S//Li-In reached 246.2
and 435.1 Ω, respectively, as depicted in Figure 2d. The Nyquist
plot consists of a semicircle at the high-medium frequency seg-
ment related to the bulk resistance of the Li7P3S11 SE electrolyte
(Rs) and charge transfer resistance (Rct).[49–51] A straight line in
the low-frequency region represents Li+ diﬀusion related to the
Warburg impedance.[52] Since the same electrolyte is applied, the
impedance variation in the high-medium frequency region is
mainly due to the charge transfer impedance at the interface.[52]
The EIS further conﬁrms that the low charge transfer resistance
(Rct: 246.2 Ω) of Li2S-0.1In could stem not only from the high
electronic and ionic conductivity of Li2S-0.1In but also from im-
proving the Li+ diﬀusion of the interface. These results demon-
strate that using Li2S-0.1In as an active material can regulate the
electrochemical redox reaction kinetics, which would be advanta-
geous for improving the utilization of Li2S in the cell.
The long-term cycling performance of Li2S-0.1In and Li2S
cathode is further compared at 2.5 mgLi2S cm−2 at a relatively high
current, for example, 0.192 mA cm−2 (Figure 3a). Li2S-0.1In de-
livered a ﬁrst areal speciﬁc capacity of 1.53 mAh cm−2; however,
it retained 1.26 mAh cm−2 over 200 cycles with ≈99.95% aver-
age Coulumbic eﬃciency. In sharp contrast, Li2S delivered an
initial areal speciﬁc capacity of 0.65 mAh cm−2 and retained ≈
0.55 mAh cm−2 over 200 cycles. Furthermore, the electrochem-
ical performance of Li/S batteries against high active material
loading (Li2S > 5 mg cm−2) is essential for industrial applica-
tions. Thus, the application potential of Li2S-0.1In cathode de-
sign at high active material loading was also studied. The Li2S-
0.1In cathode delivered the highest areal capacity of 4.08 mAh
cm−2 at 6.6 mgLi2S cm−2 at RT, which has never been reported be-
fore (Figure 3b), and the Table S1 (Supporting Information) sum-
marizes and compares the areal capacities of the recently regis-
tered all-solid-state Li-S cells. Interestingly, Li2S-0.1In presented
an excellent areal capacity of 3.02 mAh cm−2 over 50 cycles, cor-
responding to 74.01% capacity retention. Notably, the areal ca-
pacity Li2S-0.1In cathode at 6.6 mgLi2S cm−2 is comparable to
commercial lithium-ion batteries (typically > 3mAh cm−2) at RT.
Thus, our Li2S-0.1In cathode design overcomes the severe issues,
for example, mass transport and volume expansion/contraction,
which generally aggravated at higher loading, leading to poor
performance.

ing and discharging.[55–57] Large volume change not only destroys
the cathode material’s structural stability but also leads to cracks
and triple-phase interfacial contact loss, eventually deteriorating
battery performance. Therefore, in situ TEM measurement per-
formed the direct and real-time observation for volume change
in the cathode during de-lithiation and lithiation. The controlled
experiment was conducted ﬁrstly in Figure S5 (Supporting Infor-
mation) to rule out other factors (such as electron beam-induced
eﬀects, electron dose, environment factors in the column, and so

The mechanism of electrochemical redox reactions in solid-
state Li/S battery integrated by Li2S-LixIn2S3 cathode is presented
below.
3.1. Delithiation of LixIn2S3
At initial charging, LixIn2S3 gets delithiation ﬁrst:
Li2S −LixIn2S3 →Li2S + xLi+ + xe−+ In2S3
(2)
3.2. (Electro)chemical oxidation of Li2S
Later on, Li2S undergoes a conventional electrochemical oxida-
tion reaction:
Li2S →S + 2Li+ + 2e−
(3)
3.3. Lithiation of S/In2S3
S + xLi+ + xe−+ In2S3 →Li2S −LixIn2S3
(4)
The mechanism of electrochemical redox reactions (charg-
ing/discharging) suggests that In2S3 and LixIn2S3 are the re-
dox mediators exploiting the performance of Li2S-LixIn2S3-based
solid-state Li/S batteries.
4. Conclusion
In conclusion, a novel Li2S-LixIn2S3 was realized via a simple
high-energy ball milling and applied as an active material for
all-solid-state Li/S batteries. LixIn2S3 is highlighted as both a
charge-carrier (Li+ and e−) mobility mediator and redox medi-
ator for Li2S activation, which guarantees eﬃcient electronic and
ionic transportation at the cathode interface and inhibits the huge
volumetric change in ASSLSBs. In situ TEM observes limited
volume changes of the Li2S-LixIn2S3 cathode. Thus, the Li2S-
LixIn2S3 cathode designed with Li7P3S11 SE realizes excellent
electrochemical performance, for example, the initial areal capac-
ity of 3.47 mAh cm−2 at 4.0 mgLi2S cm−2 with 78% utilization of
Li2S. The solid-state cell with Li2S-LixIn2S3 cathode exhibits a re-
markable rate capability up to 0.64 mA cm−2. Besides, solid-state
cells with Li2S-LixIn2S3 cathode delivered an excellent capacity
retention of 82.35% over 200 cycles at 0.192 mA cm−2. More im-
portantly, Li2S-LixIn2S3 gave the highest initial areal capacity of
4.08 mAh cm−2 and retained ≈74.01% capacity retention over 50
cycles versus 6.6 mgLi2S cm−2 at 0.192 mA cm−2 at RT, never re-
ported before. This work demonstrates that an interfacial redox
mediator with acceptable charge carriers in the cathode could re-
lease the volumetric change of the cathode and boost reaction
kinetic, thus ensuring excellent electrochemical performance in
ASSLSBs.
