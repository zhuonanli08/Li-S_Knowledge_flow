A nitrogen-doped carbon skeleton derived from biomass as
conductive agent for electrochemically stable cathode of
all-solid-state lithium-sulfur batteries

a b s t r a c t
All-solid-state lithiumesulfur batteries (ASSLSBs) have attracted much attention for the next-generation
storage system due to their high energy density and safety. However, the oxidative and reductive
decomposition of sulﬁde electrolyte during cycling causes limited application of ASSLSBs. Herein, we
design a porous nitrogen-doped carbon skeleton derived from biomass material. The porous design
minimizes the adverse effect of volume change of sulfur. Meanwhile, the introduction of N-doped carbon
skeleton improves the electronic conductivity of composite cathode and the sulfur on this skeleton re-
duces direct contact between the electronic conductor and sulﬁde electrolyte, thus effectively promoting
rapid electron transfer and inhibiting electron aggregation on the surface of electrolyte. Therefore, such
an efﬁcient electron transfer network notably suppresses the irreversible decomposition of electrolyte
and enhances its cycling stability during charge/discharge process. The composite cathode delivers initial
discharge capacity of 1145.9 mAh/g and a capacity retention of 88.14% after 100 cycles at 0.1 C.

1. Introduction
With the development of clean energy, utilizing high perfor-
mance energy storage devices is of vital importance [1e8].
Lithiumesulfur batteries (LSBs) have drawn extensive attention
due to its high theoretical energy density (2600 Wh/kg) [9e13]. In
addition, the sulfur as cathode material of LSBs is low in its cost and
rich in its reserves [14,15].
In liquid electrolyte system, there are intractability problems
such as shuttle effects [16e18], evident volume change between
sulfur and discharge products (Li2S) [19e21], as well as their
insulation [4,5,22e24]. All these lead to low sulfur utilization and
rapid battery capacity decay. Besides, organic electrolyte tends to
trigger safety problems, such as burning and explosion [25,26]. The
use of solid-state electrolyte can fundamentally avoid the above-
mentioned issues [27,28]. With its high ionic conductivity, sulﬁde
electrolytes are often employed in the production of all-solid-state
lithiumesulfur
batteries
(ASSLSBs)
[29e35].
However,
the
components of the cathode are in solid-solid contact without the
inﬁltration of organic electrolyte, sulfur and its discharge product
(Li2S) are insulated, and sulﬁde electrolyte decomposes itself dur-
ing cycling. All these lead to poor electrochemical performance of
ASSLSBs.
To date, properly designed triple-phase interfaces can construct
excellent electron conduction pathways among the components of
the cathode [36e42]. A co-axial carbon nanotube/sulfur composite
showed fast electron transport due to the intimate contacts be-
tween the active materials and electronic network, realizing
enhanced conversion kinetics [43]. Besides, constructing good
conductive frameworks by the addition of electron conductor to
cathode can further increase the electronic conductivity of the
cathode [44e46]. Gao et al. proposed an intercalationeconversion
composite cathode by distributing sulfur evenly on electroactive
Nb18W16O93 and conductive carbon nanotubes. Nb18W16O93 serve
as a Li-ion/electron conductor, achieving excellent reversible
discharge-charge behavior [44]. Recently, dry-ﬁlm processes have
been considered for the designing of composite sulfur cathodes.
Wu et al. developed a new dry binder styreneebutadiene rubber.
The solid electrolytes membrane prepared using this processed
styreneebutadiene rubber exhibits ultra-high ionic conductivity,

Sulﬁde electrolytes (SEs) have relatively narrow electrochemical
stability voltage window, and the oxidative and reductive decom-
position of them have been experimentally observed [48]. How-
ever, this irreversible decomposition of Li6PS5Cl electrolyte in
cathode is usually overlooked. Meng et al. elucidated the redox
behavior and the mechanisms of Li6PS5Cl irreversible decomposi-
tion. And this study puts forward that developing excellent
conductive networks to maintain an electronic pathway between
cathode particles with minimal exposure to the SE is an effective
way to reduce its decomposition kinetics [49]. Therefore, rational
design of S/C composite is the key to reducing irreversible elec-
trolyte decomposition and achieving stable cycling performance of
ASSLSBs.
In this present work, we designed a porous carbon skeleton with
homogeneous in situ nitrogen-doped and compounded with sulfur
by melt-diffusion method. When the S/C/Li6PS5Cl composite cath-
ode coupled with Li6PS5Cl electrolyte layer and Li/In anode, it ex-
hibits encouraging long cycling stability and rate capability.
Interfacial characterization demonstrates that this carbon material
effectively suppressing irreversible electrolyte decomposition. This
work is vital to conﬁrm the necessary to maintain the SE stability of
the cathode for ASSLSBs.
2. Experimental
5 g soybean ﬂour and 5 g KOH were ground uniformly with a
mortar. The prepared sample was heated to 700 C at a rate of 5 C/
min and held for 1.5 h in a nitrogen environment. Then, furnace was
placed to cool down to room temperature, while the sample was
washed by 5 M hydrochloric acid to pH 7, and dried at 60 C for 12 h
to obtain the soybean ﬂour carbon (FBC). The control sample, ﬂour
carbon (FC) was processed from ﬂour carbon with less nitrogen
content than FBC.
Sulfurecarbon composites were prepared by melt-diffusion
method. 3 g sublimated sulfur and 1 g FBC were ground evenly.
The mixture was transferred to a reaction jar and kept at 155 C for
12 h to obtain FBC/S. FC/S was prepared under the same conditions.
The Li6PS5Cl electrolyte was purchased from Ganfeng Lithium Co.,
Ltd. The composite cathode is composed of FBC/S and Li6PS5Cl in a
ratio of 7: 3. The assembly processes took place in an argon at-
mosphere glove box (H2O < 0.1 ppm, O2 < 0.1 ppm), then ball milled
at 300 rad/min for 12 h. Finally, the composite electrode material
FBC/S/SE was obtained, and the control group was denoted as FC/S/
SE.
The microstructures of the samples are characterized by X-ray
diffraction (XRD, Rigaku D/max 2550 PC) and CuKa radiation
(1.54178 Å) in a 2q range from 10 to 80. Raman spectroscopy are
characterized using a Renishaw Raman microscope under 532 nm
laser excitation. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (Thermo Scien-
tiﬁc K-Alphaþ, Al Ka X-ray) was characterized using an ESCA-
LAB_250Xi spectrometer. Surface topography and microstructure
were characterized using scanning electron microscope (SEM,
Hitachi SU8010) and transmission electron microscope (JEOL JEM-
2100F). The sulfur content in the sulfurecarbon samples was
determined using thermos gravimetric analysis (Netzsch STA 449C
thermal analyzer).
The solid electrolyte layer was obtained by cold pressing 100 mg
of Li6PS5Cl at 150 MPa for 1 min in a mold with an inner diameter of
10 mm. FBC/S/SE was evenly distributed on the electrolyte side and
cold pressed at 350 MPa for 3 min. The mass loading of sulfur was
1.2 mg/cm. On the other side, an indium-lithium two layered sheet
with a thickness of 100 mm was successively placed as the anode of
the ASSLSBs. Compared with lithium metal, indium layer can
stabilize the interface between electrolyte and anode. The battery
assembly process was completed in an argon atmosphere glove box
(H2O < 0.1 ppm, O2 < 0.1 ppm).
The ionic conductivity of the Li6PS5Cl was tested by a multi-
channel electrochemical work station (PARSTAT MC) at frequencies
from 1 MHz to 10 Hz with temperatures of 298e373 K. 150 mg of
Li6PS5Cl was pressed at 150 MPa for 1 min in a 10 mm diameter
mold, ﬂanked with charcoal-coated aluminum foil as blocking
electrodes and collector collectors. In order to test the electron
conductivity of the positive electrode, 60 mg FBC/S/SE was cold
pressed at 350 MPa for 3 min and tested by applying 1 mV direct
current (DC) voltage. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) was performed in the
potential range of 0.8e2.4 V (Li/Liþ) at a scanning speed of 0.1 mV/s.
Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy of ASSLSBs was tested at
frequencies from 1 MHz to 0.1 Hz. The ASSLSBs were tested for
galvanostatic charge and discharge behavior on the multichannel
battery test system (LAND CT-2001A) in the voltage range of
0.8e2.4 V at 25 C.

3. Results and discussion
Fig. 1a and d reveal the scanning electron microscope (SEM)
images of FBC and FC. Due to the activation of KOH, FBC and FC are
both cross-linked network structures with lots of holes about 1e2
mm. The transmission electron microscope (TEM) images in Fig. 1b
and e exhibit that FBC and FC have no obvious lattice stripes and are
amorphous structure. In Fig. 1c and f, it can be seen that FBC and FC
are both short-range ordered and long-range disordered structures.
Nevertheless, the lattice is even less orderly in FBC. In Fig. S1, FBC
has higher degree of the ration of D peak to G peak (1.05) than FC
(0.91), providing that it has more defects [51].
Fig. S2 presents the nitrogen adsorptionedesorption isothermal
variation curves and pore size distribution curves of FBC and FC.
Higher nitrogen content results in more porosity and a larger
speciﬁc surface area in FBC. The FBC with high speciﬁc surface area
has high availability load of sulfur, and the adverse effect of the
insulation of sulfur on the electrochemical performance of the
ASSLSBs can be reduced. The average diameter of pores in FBC is 2.5
nm. The large number of mesopores can be better to accommodate
sulfur and decrease the volume expansion and contraction during
cycling. The energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDX) spectrum proves
that nitrogen is homogeneously doped on the carbon substrate (Fig.
1g and Fig. S3). In the X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern (Fig. 1h), the
peaks at 2q of 24 and 40 correspond to (002) (100) crystallo-
graphic planes of the amorphous structure of carbon material [52].

further analyze the elements of the materials. In the full spectrum
(Fig. S4), three peaks can be clearly observed that correspond to C
(284.7 eV), N (400.0 eV), and O (532.5 eV). Speciﬁc molar ratio is
shown in Table S1, in which FBC has higher nitrogen content. As
shown in Figs. 1i, j, N 1s high-resolution spectra can be divided into
three peaks, namely graphite N (400.7 eV), pyrrolic N (399.8 eV),

At the Fermi level (Ef), none of the DOS values are 0, proving that
they all have metallicity. The electronic conductivity is related to
the DOS near Ef [54]. At the Fermi level, the greater the density of
electronic states, the stronger the electronic conductivity of the
material. Among them, the DOS of nitrogen-doped carbon mate-
rials are higher than that of graphitic carbon, which indicates that
nitrogen doping can improve the electronic conductivity of the
carbon materials. Comparing the three types of nitrogen-doped
carbon, the DOS is the highest for the graphitic N-doped carbon,
followed by pyridinic N-doped nitrogen carbon, and the smallest is
pyrrolic N-doped carbon. It shows that the graphite N-doped car-
bon material has the highest electrical conductivity. The above
theoretical calculations show that nitrogen doping, especially
graphitic N doping, can improve the electrical conductivity of the
composite cathode, thus forming a better electrically conductive
network and maintaining a rapid electron pathway between the
active material particles.
To investigate the electrochemical stability of solid electrolyte in
the cathodes during cycling, XPS tests are conducted to pristine
Li6PS5Cl and the surfaces of FBC/S/SE and FC/S/SE cathodes after 50
cycles (Fig. 5). The XPS spectrum of pristine Li6PS5Cl electrolyte can
be divided into doublet peaks, which is PS43 thiophosphate units
[55]. After 50 cycles, the S 2p XPS spectrum displays newly formed
peaks, which is the signal about Li2S at 160.1 eV and P2S5. While Li2S
is sulfur discharge product and partial electrolyte decomposition
product, P2S5 is the irreversible product of electrolyte decomposi-
tion [49]. All these testify that the inevitable decomposition of
electrolyte after cycling. In stark contrast, the relative intensity of
P2S5 signal is greatly weakened in the FBC/S/SE cathode compared
with FC/S/SE, proving that the degree of decomposition is small. For
the P 2p spectrum, it emerges a doublet peaks at 132.8 eV and
133.3 eV after cycling which are related to insulating irreversible
decomposition products (P2S5), in agreement with the S 2p spectra.
The XPS results show the signiﬁcant difference between the FBC/S/
SE and FC/S/SE cathodes in ASSLSBs after 50 cycles. In the P 2p
spectrum of FC/S/SE cathode, there is a stronger P2S5 peaks, proving
its greater degree of electrolyte decomposition. In Fig. 5c, little
changes can be found in the Cl 2p spectra. Presumably due to the
high electronic conductivity of the FBC's reticulated texture,
achieving the rapid electron conduction between the components
of the cathode and inhibiting electron aggregation on the surface of
electrolyte. Besides, the uniform distribution of sulfur on the sur-
face of carbon skeleton reduces the contact between the carbon
material and Li6PS5Cl. Li6PS5Cl has less exposure to electronically
conductive surfaces, and the kinetics of electrolyte decomposition
can be signiﬁcantly reduced without a sufﬁcient electronic pathway
for electrolyte redox to occur [49]. Therefore, this efﬁcient electron
transfer
network
reduces
the
irreversible
decomposition
of
Li6PS5Cl, which can effectively improve the cycling performance of
the ASSLSBs with FBC/S/SE cathode.
4. Conclusion
In conclusion, a porous nitrogen-doped carbon skeleton derived
from biomass material has been designed and is used as an efﬁcient
electron transfer network for sulfur cathode of ASSLSBs. The porous
design minimizes the adverse effect of volume change of sulfur. The
N-doped carbon skeleton improves the electronic conductivity of
composite cathode, effectively promoting rapid electron transfer.
Besides, it reduces direct contact between electronic conductor and
SE, inhibiting electron aggregation on the surface of electrolyte.
Therefore,
the
carbon
skeleton
suppressed
the
irreversible
decomposition of electrolyte and the ASSLSBs with the FBC/S/SE
electrode delivers a initial discharge capacity of 1145.9 mAh/g and a
capacity retention of 88.14% after 100 cycles at 0.1 C. Overall, this
porous nitrogen-doped carbon skeleton would be a promising
choice for the cathode design of ASSLSBs.
