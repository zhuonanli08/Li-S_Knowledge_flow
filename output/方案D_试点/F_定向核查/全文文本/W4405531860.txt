All-solid-state Li-S batteries (ASSLSBs) are more attractive owing to their achievable superior energy den-
sity at a reasonable cost and the solid electrolyte (SE) utilization mitigating the widely recognized poly-
sulﬁde shuttle problem. While the volume expansion ( 80%) that occurs during the initial transformation 
of sulfur to lithium sulﬁde induces mechanical stress, this can be avoided by using Li2S as a cathode, 
which also permits the anode-free cell design. However, the high oxidation energy barrier of Li2S cathode 
during the charging step limits its application in commercial devices. Redox mediators have been exten-
sively used to reduce the oxidation energy barrier of Li2S to the sulfur conversation and boost the rever-
sible kinetics of the conversion reaction. In this review, we have summarized the available redox 
mediators for Li2S cathode in ASSLSBs and its working mechanism. Moreover, we have proposed novel 
strategies and guidelines for designing effective redox mediators to boost the reversible conversion 
reaction.

Li2O/Li2O2 and S/Li2S2 [18,21–23].

2. Working principle of RMs 
RMs in ASSLSBs serve as the carriers of the electrons that 
enhance the transmission of electrons between the active material 
and conducting agent, leading to enhanced battery performance 
[29,30]. The working mechanism RM follows two steps during 
the mediation process which are the chemical and electro-
chemical process (Fig. 2a, b). During charging, the chemical reac-
tion between RM and Li2S takes place, the oxidized RM (RMox) gets 
reduced to RMred, and chemically oxidizes Li2S to lithium polysul-
ﬁdes (LiPSs). Then, the reduced form of RMred returns to its initial 
stage by releasing electrons to the current collector by undergoing 
electrochemical reactions. Overall, the RMs facilitate the electron 
transfer between the current collector and isolated Li2S particles, 
enhancing the redox kinetics of the Li2S cathode. The important 
requirement of the material to act as the RM is that the redox 
potential of the RM should be higher than that of Li2S. RM is being 
utilized in two distinct approaches for the Li2S cathode. The com-
posite strategy involves the physical mixing of the redox mediator 
(RM) with Li2S cathode particles resulting in a composite structure. 
This approach is constrained by the limited contact area between 
the RM and Li2S particles, impeding electronic transportation 
within the composite and diminishing the Li2S conversion efﬁ-
ciency. Conversely, the core-shell approach entails uniformly cov-
ering the Li2S cathode with the RM. This approach improves the 
effective contact area between Li2S and RM, hence considerably 
enhancing the reversible conversion efﬁciency of the Li2S cathode 
(Fig. 2c).

4. Summary and future perspective 
In this review, we summarized the applications of RMs for 
improving the redox kinetics of Li2S cathode in ASSLSBs. The avail-
able reports indicated that the utilization of RMs on the cathode 
side effectively promotes the Li2S oxidation process by suppressing 
the activation energy barrier of the Li2S oxidation during the initial 
charging process and improves the reversibility of the redox reac-
tion. However, the efﬁcacy of RMs in ASLSBs is not well addressed 
for commercial application owing to the limited information avail-
able about the practical application. Therefore, we proposed a few 
important factors that need to be paid more attention to for 
implementing the RMs widely in the practical consideration 
(Fig. 5).

4.1. Design strategies of RMs for Li2S cathode for ASSLSBs 
4.1.1. Redox mediator with suitable redox potential 
The selection of RMs is crucial in achieving desirable perfor-
mance in the ASSBs. Li2S electrodes encounter difﬁculties in activa-
tion due to their inadequate electronic conductivity in the 
composite cathode. The oxidation reaction is restricted to speciﬁc 
regions of the active particles that are in direct contact with elec-
tron and ion transfer channels. Consequently, this mechanism 
requires a substantial overpotential, which leads to a cell capacity 
signiﬁcantly lower than the theoretical value. The process of 
selecting the proper RMs can be difﬁcult since many competing 
demands must be met. The key requirements and justiﬁcation for 
ﬁnding the RMs as follows. The equilibrium potential of RM is 
important from a thermodynamic point of view when choosing 
the proper RMs. Speciﬁcally, the thermodynamic oxidation poten-
tial of RM should be higher than that of Li2S to ensure that it can 
chemically oxidize the Li2S cathode. For effective oxidation of 
Li2S, the redox potential of the RM is to be as close to 2.8 V vs.

Li/Li+ . Moreover, the RM must spontaneously involve the chemical 
reaction with active material during charge-discharge. Further, the 
ideal RM should possess high electrochemical reversibility and 
minimized reactivity with other battery components. Moreover, 
RMs should exhibit exceptional chemical stability against corro-
sion caused by very reactive sulfur intermediates. Currently, the 
selection of RMs in ASSLSBs is limited. The redox-active organic 
and organometallic compounds, whose redox potential can be 
modiﬁed via functional groups or structural design, need further 
attention

4.1.2. Stability of RMs 
The chemical and electrochemical stability of RM in ASSLSBs is 
critical for ensuring efﬁcient charge transfer, minimizing deteriora-
tion, preserving compatibility with solid electrolytes, and sustain-
ing long-term battery performance, safety, and reliability. The 
electrochemical stability windows of solid electrolytes are con-
strained. To prevent electrolyte deterioration or unfavorable side 
effects, the redox potential of RMs must be within the range of 
solid electrolyte. For instance, the RM needs to be chosen to func-
tion below the oxidative potential of the sulﬁde solid electrolyte 
because sulﬁde-based solid electrolytes are more susceptible to 
oxidative degradation. Moreover, the RM must always be com-
pletely regenerated following each charging or discharging cycle 
and remain non-consumable. High stability should be exhibited 
by their redox reactions, preventing any side reactions apart from

the designated chemical and electrochemical processes for achiev-
ing stable and enhanced performance.

4.1.3. Particle size 
The particle size of Li2S cathode and RM plays a vital role in 
determining the reversibility of the electrochemical process. Since 
the electrical conductivity and Li-diffusion kinetics of the Li2S par-
ticle is less, the large particle size could lead to the incomplete 
extraction of Li from the Li2S particle during the initial charge pro-
cess which reduces the complete utilization of the Li2S particle in 
the electrochemical process. Moreover, the interaction between 
the redox mediator and the electrode materials can be inﬂuenced 
by the size and dispersion of the electrode particles. The electro-
chemical performance of the mediator is improved when the par-
ticles have a uniform distribution because it makes the mediator 
more accessible to the active material. Therefore, ﬁnding the opti-
mal size of Li2S is essential to completely utilize the capacity of the 
Li2S cathode during the cycling process. 
4.1.4. Active reaction sites 
The reactive surface area of RM with the Li2S cathode material 
impacts the Li2S oxidation performance during the initial charge 
process. In general, the materials with a high surface area are more 
suitable for improving the redox kinetics and electron transport 
during the electrochemical reaction between Li2S and RM, activat-
ing the redox process, and increasing the overall utilization of Li2S. 
Also, a redox mediator with a greater surface area guarantees 
improved contact with the Li2S particles. As Li2S has a low conduc-
tivity, ensuring that a greater portion of its surface is in contact 
with a redox mediator increases the amount of Li2S that can partic-
ipate in the electrochemical reactions. This results in improved 
overall energy density and a higher practical capacity. Moreover, 
the increased surface area guarantees more efﬁcient redox reac-
tions, which mitigates polarization within the Li2S cathode during 
cycling. This reduces the overpotential and enhances energy efﬁ-
ciency and density. Therefore, the RM with a high surface could 
be designed to achieve high performance in Li2S cathode contain-
ing ASSLSBs. 
4.1.5. Utilization method of RMs in ASSLSBs 
The utilization strategy of RMs plays a vital role in the electro-
chemical reversibility of Li2S oxidation. The random distribution of 
RM in the composite strategy of the Li2S cathode limits the avail-
ability of the reaction sites for the electrochemical oxidation of 
Li2S particles. Also, the insulating properties Li2S conﬁne their elec-
troreduction and electrooxidation reactions to the active particle 
areas that are intimately linked to electron and ion transfer chan-
nels. There are particular difﬁculties in developing a sustained 
cycle reaction bypass in ASSSBs when the RM and the electrode 
active material are both solid. Innovative strategies are needed to 
guarantee effective electron and ion movement while preserving 
chemical compatibility and reactivity due to the immobility of 
solid components. In solid-state systems, the redox mediator and 
active material must interchange both electrons and ions. MIEC 
materials offer dual conductivity to facilitate the RM redox mech-
anism. In addition, the core-shell design strategy involves uni-
formly coating the Li2S cathode with the RM which increases the 
effective contact area between Li2S and RM, hence signiﬁcantly 
improving the ionic/electronic transport and reversible conversion 
efﬁciency of the Li2S cathode. Therefore, the adaptation core-shell 
strategy could be a viable technique for improving the reversible 
kinetics of the Li2S cathode. Although solid-state diffusion is intrin-
sically slower than liquid-phase diffusion, it can be accelerated by 
minimizing diffusion lengths and optimizing the intrinsic conduc-
tivity of RMs. Overall, the above-mentioned design strategies 
speciﬁcally address the issues associated with the immobility of 
solid components, hence assuring efﬁcient and constant redox 
cycling in ASSLSBs.

4.1.6. Electrode composition 
The composition of the electrode materials signiﬁcantly inﬂu-
ences the electronic conductivity of the electrode. In sulfur cath-
odes with low electronic conductivity, a high percentage of 
conducting carbon (20%–40%) has been utilized to improve the 
electronic conductivity of the electrode. However, the usage of 
excess carbon triggers the side reaction and reduces the energy 
density of the battery. On the other hand, the Li2S cathode exhibits 
better electronic conductivity than the sulfur cathode. Thus, using 
a similar electrode composition with a high percent of conductive 
carbon is not ideal for Li2S cathode and the electrode composition 
needs to be optimized to improve the energy density of the battery. 
Also, when designing the RM, we need to consider and design it 
with high electronic conductivity to mitigate the challenges of con-
ducting carbon additives in ASSBs. 
4.2. In-depth analysis of reaction mechanism RMs in ASSLSBs 
RMs are improving the revisable redox kinetics of the Li2S cath-
ode by undergoing an additional reaction pathway. This cyclic pro-
cess enables RMs to behave as an electron carrier, which improves 
electron transmission between active materials and conductive 
substrates. However, the reaction mechanism of RM and its inﬂu-
ence on the Li2S oxidation potential have not proved well. More-
over, understanding the working mechanisms of RMs under 
battery operating conditions is crucial for improving the long-
term stability and efﬁciency of RMs in Li2S-based ASSLSBs. Using 
modern in-situ characterization technologies for in-depth studies 
can provide vital insights into the working mechanism of RMs 
and their challenges. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), 
time-of-ﬂight secondary ion mass spectrometry (TOF-SIMS), and 
Raman spectroscopy have all proven useful for detecting degrada-
tion mechanisms. Also, innovative methods such as cryogenic elec-
tron microscopy (Cryo-EM), are especially signiﬁcant because they 
reveal detailed interfacial processes while also minimizing electron 
irradiation damage, maintaining the structural integrity of delicate 
samples. In addition, the chemistry of the RM and the particular 
impact of the RM on the Li2S oxidation are further conﬁrmed by 
cryo-electron energy loss spectroscopy (cryo-EELS) mapping. Fur-
thermore, in situ and operando investigations using modern tech-
nologies such as scanning transmission electron microscopy 
(STEM) combined with electron energy loss spectroscopy (EELS) 
are critical. These approaches provide real-time insights into the 
dynamic mechanisms that drive charge and discharge, resulting 
in a comprehensive understanding of the working mechanism of 
the RM. 
Although the Li2S cathode addresses the volume expansion 
issues of the sulfur cathode, the volume shrinkage of Li2S particles 
during the initial charge can lead to contact loss between particles 
in the composite cathode which could deteriorate the electro-
chemical performance of the battery. Therefore, in-situ stress mon-
itoring studies must be carried out to investigate the volume 
variation induced problems. Also, more research attention needs 
to be paid to designing a stress-free cell design for achieving 
long-term performance. 
4.3. Application of theoretical and simulation studies 
Several factors, including stability, redox potentials, and cat-
alytic activity of the RM are difﬁcult to evaluate because of the dif-
ﬁcult experimental circumstances. Recently, density functional

theory (DFT) calculations have been extensively utilized in Li-S 
batteries for the calculation of absorption properties of lithium 
polysulﬁdes through adsorption energy calculation, catalytic prop-
erties of polysulﬁde conversion material through activation energy 
calculation, and the polysulﬁde diffusion kinetics through the Li2S 
decomposition barrier. Using ﬁrst-principles-based high through-
put computational screening with a narrow focus on physical fac-
tors offers a feasible way to address the experimental issues. 
Furthermore, it would be beneﬁcial to create more general design 
guidelines for effective RM discovery, since this might streamline 
and accelerate upcoming experiments. Therefore, more DFT calcu-
lation is needed to ﬁnd the suitable RM for ASSLSBs.
