ABSTRACT: The retentive functional intimate contact at the
solid electrolyte/cathode interface and among the cathode
components, for example, solid electrolyte, a conductive additive,
and active material (S/Li2S), is essential for high-performance
solid-state lithium−sulfur batteries. Currently, thiophosphate-based
electrolytes are plagued by failure at the interfaces due to the
intrinsically narrow electrochemical stability window, which in
principle, derive uneven irreversible redox reactions at the triple
point of contact, which result in sulfur- and phosphorus-enriched
interphases (e.g., Li3P, P2SX, S, Li2Sn, etc.), retard Li+ conduction,
and increase the interface resistance. Herein, structural tuning of
Li7P3S11 was done using the In2S3 dopant, and the designed Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92 electrolyte presented better σLi+ of 2.9 mS cm−1 and
enhanced air stability @ RT. Furthermore, In2S3 broadened the electrochemical stability window and suppressed the redox
decomposition of Li7P3S11 at the interface in the Li2S-composite cathode layer, ensuring effective (ionic/electronic) conduction.
Therefore, the Li2S/Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92/Li−In cell offers a high discharge capacity of 946.75 mA h g−1 with an ∼100% average
Coulombic efficiency over 30 cycles. Moreover, the cell with Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92 presented a low interfacial resistance of 127
compared to 383 Ω for counterparts over 30 cycles, which could be benefitted from suppressed redox decomposition of the solid
electrolyte and long-lasting intimate contact at the triple point of contact. Thus, the projected doping strategy developed a sulfide
electrolyte to address the chemical/electrochemical stabilities and redox decomposition of sulfide electrolytes for the next-generation
all-solid-state technologies.
KEYWORDS: Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92 glass ceramic, air stability, low redox decomposition, stable solid electrolyte/cathode interface,
all-solid-state lithium−sulfur batteries
1. INTRODUCTION
Worldwide electrification requires a green energy-storage
system to realize high energy density, longer cycle life, and
superior intrinsic safety. Therefore, rechargeable batteries or
fuel cells are promising next-generation battery technologies.1
Among battery technologies, lithium−sulfur (Li/S) batteries
with high energy density2 are acknowledged as one of the most
promising candidates to ensure the power starting from low to
heavy locomotives. However, the commercialization of Li/S
cells is yet restricted by some problems, for example, shuttling
of polysulfide intermediates (Li2Sx: 4 ≤x ≤8) declines the
cyclic performance and Coulombic efficiency of the Li/S cells
badly. In this regard, numerous strategies have been
endeavored to alleviate the abovementioned issues. Therefore,
the installation of inorganic superionic conductors would
finally eliminate the polysulfide shuttling and in principle could
enhance the capacity and performance of Li/S batteries.3
The solid electrolytes (SEs) are a vital element of solid-state
batteries (SSBs) and can be classified as follows: (1) solid
polymer electrolytes and (2) inorganic electrolytes.4−7
However, SEs have not been widely employed due to their
low σLi+, narrow electrochemical voltage window, and high
electrode/electrolyte interface resistance.8 Thus, an ideal SE
must have high σLi+ (>1 mS cm−1), broad electrochemical
voltage window, and chemical/electrochemical stability with
electrodes.9,10 Among the inorganic superionic conductors,

2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
2.1. Material Preparation. Li7P3S11-xIn2S3 (x = 0, 0.005, 0.01,
and 0.02) glass ceramics were designed by ball-milling (Fritsch,
Pulverisette 7) and a subsequent heating strategy. P2S5 (99%,
Macklin), Li2S (99.9%, Alfa Aesar), and In2S3 (99%, Macklin) were
added to an Ar gas-filled 45 mL zirconia pot with 5 zirconia balls (4
mm in diameter) in the proper molar ratio. The milling operation was
executed for 45 h @ 510 rpm. Overheating was avoided by topping
the machine for an appropriate time. The milled amorphous powders
were heated @ 280 °C for 2 h to obtain glass-ceramic electrolytes. All
the experimental steps were performed in an argon-filled glovebox
(H2O < 0.1 ppm, O2 < 0.1 ppm).
2.2. Structure Characterization. Powder X-ray diffraction
(XRD) was carried out using a Bruker D8 instrument with Cu Kα
radiation (1.54 Å at 45 kV and 40 mA) from 10 to 60° @ 5° min−1.
The Raman spectroscopic experiment was conducted on a Thermo
Fisher XDR at a 532 nm wavelength from 200 to 600 cm−1. The
element analysis was carried out with X-ray photoelectron spectros-
copy (XPS) (ESCALAB-250Xi) with Al Kα X-ray. The XPS data were
analyzed using XPS Peak software, and the binding energies were
revised based on the C 1s peak at 284.8 eV. Solid-state nuclear
magnetic resonance (SS-NMR) spectra were conducted on a Bruker
Ascend 700M spectrometer and a 4 mm probe was operated at
different frequencies to the structural environment of lithium and
phosphorus atoms. Sulfide electrolytes (about 100 mg) were packed
into a 1.9 mm cylindrical zirconia MAS rotor in a glovebox (argon-
filled) to avoid the reaction with oxygen and moisture and revolved at
15,000 Hz. The recycle delay was 60 s to get the quantitative spectra
of glass-ceramic SEs. The amount of hydrogen sulfide (H2S)
produced from solid-state samples was measured against the relative
humidity of 20%. SE powder (0.1 g) and H2S gas sensor (GX-2009,
Riken Keiki Co, Ltd, Japan) were packed in a jar of volume ∼2880
cm3 filled with moisture @ 25 °C.
2.3. Electrochemical Characterization. The ionic conductiv-
ities of cold-pressed solid electrolytes were measured on a Solartron
1400/1470E cell test system. Stainless steel disks were used as the
blocking electrodes on both sides of the pellets, and the alternating
current (AC) impedance method was performed in the frequency
range of 0.1 Hz to 1 MHz with an amplitude of 10 mV. The
electrochemical window of electrolytes was evaluated by cyclic
voltammetry (CV). The test was conducted on a Li/SE/SE-
conductive carbon (VGCF) cell (75:25 wt %) on a multichannel
electrochemical workstation (Solartron 1470E). The CV curves were
recorded in the potential range from 0 to 5.0 V with the sweep rate of
0.1 mV s−1. Electrochemical performances of ASSLBs were

and pressed again at 120 MPa. Thus, the produced triple-layer cell
was sandwiched between stainless steel current collectors. The
loading of active Li2S is ∼2.5 mg cm−2.
3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

state nuclear magnetic resonance (SS-NMR) is extremely

The ionic conductivity is a key parameter for estimating the
applicability of SEs. The σLi+ of the Li7P3S11-xIn2S3 (x = 0,
0.005, 0.01, and 0.02) glass ceramics were evaluated by AC
impedance spectroscopy. Therefore, Li+ conductivities and the
corresponding Arrhenius plots of the Li7P3S11 and
Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92 electrolytes are equated, as shown in
Figure 2e,f, respectively. σLi+ of pristine Li7P3S11 was about
1.5 mS cm−1 at R.T. Next, increasing the amount of In2S3 (X =
0.005) enhances the σLi+ up to 2.2 mS cm−1, reaching the
maximum value of 2.9 mS cm−1 against x = 0.01 and then
decreasing to 1.3 mS cm−1 on further increasing In2S3 up to x
= 0.02. It is noteworthy that the σLi+ of Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92 is

is attributed to a bigger ionic radius, In3+ (0.92 Å), in sharp
contrast to P5+ (0.35 Å), expanding the transport channel for
Li-ion migration.25,41 Meanwhile, the transfer ability of Li+ in
units can be strengthened owing to the substitution of P5+ by
In3+ with the lower valence, implying better σLi+. Besides, the
In−S bond is longer compared to P−S bonding, which would
further expand the Li+ conduction path to ease Li+ conduction.

improved by doping, the voltage range of the actual charging
and discharging process still exceeds its intrinsic stability
window. That is, electrolyte degradation is inevitable during
the subsequent battery cycle. The redox reactions of sulfide
SEs in composite cathodes significantly influence the electronic
and ionic conduction at the triple-phase interface among active
materials, conductive additives, and SEs.47 Therefore, it is
critical to explore the decomposition behavior of sulfide
electrolytes. Figure S3ab shows the initial five charge/discharge

the first cycle delivers a discharge capacity of 241 mA h g−1.
Also, after five cycles, the capacity remains at 169 mA h g−1.
Conversely, the discharge capacity of the first cycle with the

respectively. When the rate recovers to 0.064 mA cm−2, the cell
successfully recovers the initial discharge capacity.
To further understand the influence of interfacial resistances
on cycle performance, EIS was conducted on Li2S/Li7P3S11-
xIn2S3 (x = 0, 0.01)/Li−In cells before and after 30 cycles. As
shown in Figure S4, both electrodes in their original state
exhibit similar ohmic resistance curves using the same
electrolyte and electrode layers. Unlike a battery with pure
Li7P3S11 (109 Ω), it shows a smaller intercept (74 Ω) due to
the higher ionic conductivity of Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92 for the
Li2S/Li−In cell. After 30 cycles, the interface impedance for all
Li2S/Li7P3S11-xIn2S3 (x = 0, 0.01) increased, as seen in Figure
6d. For the Li2S/Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92/Li−In cell, the interfacial
impedance of the Li2S/SE only increased to 127 Ω, smaller
than 383 Ω of the Li2S/Li7P3S11/Li−In cell. The intercept at
the X-axis in the high frequency corresponds to the ohmic
resistance of the cell, which originated from the resistance of
the SE layer and electrode layers.54 The semicircle in the
middle frequency range corresponds to the interfacial
resistance in the cathode layer. The interfacial resistance in
this work mainly originates from the redox activity of sulfide
electrolytes in the cathode mixture, leading to the formation of
side reaction products at the interface with high interfacial
resistance. For cathode electrodes composed of Li2S and
Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92 nanocomposites, an appropriate amount of
doping helps stabilize the interface between the cathode and
the solid sulfide electrolyte during the long cycle, especially
when the indium (In) doping amount is 0.01. Clearly, the Li2S
and Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92 composites show remarkable electro-
chemical performances in all-solid-state batteries, which could
benefit from the following aspects: (i) improved ionic
conductivity could provide fast lithium ion diffusion, leading
to excellent power and cyclic performances; (ii) the doped
Li7P3S11 not only provides a stable interface between Li2S and
sulfide electrolyte but also ensures the reversibility of
electrolyte decomposition, thus avoiding the accumulation of
poor conductivity byproducts and maintaining high cycling
stability.
The interfacial stability between the Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92
electrolyte and Li metal was evaluated by the galvanostatic
charge/discharge test of Li symmetric cells. As depicted in
Figure S5, the Li/Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92/Li symmetric cell can
deliver a relatively stable cycle for 100 h, although the
polarization voltage gradually increases with the cycle time. It
can also explain the slightly higher inhibition of lithium
dendrite because the pure Li/Li7P3S11/Li battery will short-
circuit immediately.
4. CONCLUSIONS
In summary, we have demonstrated that enhanced chemical
and electrochemical stability of SEs could be achieved via
replacing P5+ with In3+ in the structure of Li7P3S11. In
particular, Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92 electrolyte offers high σLi+ 2.9
mS cm−1 at room temperature and better air stability. Next, the
indium dopant incorporation broadened the electrochemical
stability window of pristine Li7P3S11 and suppressed its redox
decomposition significantly. XPS was employed to quantify the
redox products at the interface of the electrolyte−carbon
composite. It is worth noting that Li7P3S11 decomposes
irreversibly to form an interphase enriched with sulfur and
phosphorus species (S, P2Sx, or Li3P) and the insulating nature
retards ionic transport in the cathode, thus affecting the cycle
performance
of
the
battery.
Thus,
the
Li 2S/
Li6.93P2.97In0.02S10.92/Li−In cell shows outstanding electro-
chemical performance and delivers a superior discharge
capacity of 946.75 mA h g−1 over 30 cycles with ∼100%
efficiency. Next, the cell presented an excellent rate perform-
ance from 0.064 to 0.64 mA cm−2 @ RT, which is associated
with the excellent transport property after doping. Our results
show that regulating electrolytes’ decomposition behavior in
the electrode can ensure enough ion conduction paths,
effectively promoting the goal of all-solid-state lithium-sulfur
batteries.
