A B S T R A C T  
Solid-state lithium sulfur batteries constantly suffer from a poor interfacial compatibility between solid-state 
cathode/anode and electrolyte, and low critical current density of Li metal anode (< 3 mA cm−2). Here, a 
concept of a liquid cathode/anode based, room-temperature, solid-state lithium sulfur battery with low 
impedance electrode/electrolyte interfaces is presented. Due to an extremely stable liquid-solid interface with a 
high compatibility and unique properties of liquid alloy, the liquid Li-amalgam anode based symmetric cells 
show a superior electrochemical performance, and no internal short circuit occurs as they run at 55.6 mA cm−2 
for 10,000 cycles. Liquid Li2S8 polysulfide catholyte can afford a high active material loading (≥135.2 mg mL−1) 
and has a good contact with the solid-sate electrolyte. The cathode/anode and electrolyte are tightly welded 
together by the liquid-solid interfaces, and the resulting cells show high performance and good structural sta­
bility during cycling.   
1. Introduction 
With the sharply increasing demand of electrochemical energy 
storage systems for portable electronic devices and electric vehicles 
(EV), battery technology is playing an important role. However, the 
current dominant battery, lithium-ion battery, cannot meet the re­
quirements of practical application, due to its low theoretical energy 
density (≤400 Wh kg−1) [1–3]. Lithium-sulfur (Li-S) battery is widely 
considered as a potential substitute for conventional lithium-ion battery 
because of its high energy density (2600 Wh kg−1), low temperature 
performance, environmentally friendly and low cost [4–9]. Neverthe­
less, traditional Li-S battery with liquid electrolyte is limited to the low 
sulfur utilization, fast capacity degradation, low Coulombic efficiency, 
and potential safety hazards, because of the formation, diffusion and 
shuttle of soluble polysulfide intermediates between cathode and anode, 
the generation of lithium anode dendrites, and the large volume change 
of electrodes during cycling [10–12]. 
Recently, replacing liquid electrolytes with solid-state electrolytes 
(SSEs) is regarded as a significant way to solve these issues of lithium- 
sulfur battery, in terms of high mechanical strength and non- 
flammability of solid-state electrolytes which are able to suppress the 
lithium dendrite growth and improve the cell safety, and comparatively 
simple electrochemical processes in solid-state system which is capable 
of obviously alleviating the formation of polysulfide intermediates and 
avoiding the shuttle effect [13–17]. However, the poor interface sta­
bility and large interfacial impedances of anode/cathode SSE interfaces, 
and low critical current density of Li metal anode (< 3 mA cm−2) are still 
the big challenge for all-solid-state Li-S batteries [18–21]. To improve 
the interfacial compatibility between electrodes and SSEs, and reduce 
their large interfacial impedances in all-solid-state systems, various 
kinds of strategies have been proposed, such as engineering the Li-SSE 
interface, synthesizing the composites of cathode/hybrid electrolyte, 
and employing the wetting agent [22–26]. Although most of the above 
strategies are generally helpful, they are in-sufficient to overcome the 
interfacial problems between electrolyte and anode/cathode in 
solid-state batteries, because most of all-solid-state Li-S batteries need to 
be operated at an elevated temperature (>room temperature) to 
improve the ion diffusion rate, and the high working temperature easily 
induces the problems of corrosion and safety. Therefore, developing 
suitable strategies to solve the solid-solid contact issues are extremely 
required for practical all-solid-state batteries.

improve the electrochemical performance of energy storage systems, 
such as liquid-type polysulfide catholyte, Li2S8-TEGDME catholyte, Ga-

of its natural properties. Compared with the solid-solid interface, a

However, liquid electrodes are rarely introduced into the room tem­

Motivated by these results, it gives birth of the concept of novel safe 
room-temperature Li-S batteries consisting of liquid cathode, solid state 
electrolyte and liquid Li-based alloy. 
Here, we design and construct a liquid cathode/anode based solid- 
state lithium-sulfur battery with low impedance electrode/electrolyte 
interfaces, which can be operated at room temperature. Liquid Li2S8 
polysulfide catholyte is employed as cathode, liquid Li-amalgam as 
anode, and Li10GeP2S12 (LGPS) (12 mS cm–1 at 27 ◦C) [37]. as solid-state 
electrolyte. In this energy storage system, liquid Li-amalgam anode is 
capable of eliminating lithium dendrite and has a good contact with 
LGPS, exhibiting a superior electrochemical performance of the sym­
metric battery with solid-state electrolyte that can be tested up to 55.6 
mA cm−2 and run for 10,000 cycles. Also, the liquid Li-amalgam with Ni 
foam or carbon cloth can be employed as mechanical support to fabri­
cate solid-state batteries. Li2S8 polysulfide catholyte is able to afford a 
high active material loading (≥135.2 mg mL−1), avoid the issues of 
volume expansion and poor kinetics of Li2S, and reduce the interface 
impedance of cathode/electrolyte. Solid electrolyte can alleviate the 
shuttle effect of polysulfides, prevent direct contact of lithium amalgam 
with polysulfide, and solve the issues of electrolyte leakage and battery 
safety. As a result, we present and demonstrate a concept of a limited 
lithium anode supported solid-state lithium-sulfur battery with low 
impedance anode/cathode-electrolyte interfaces. 
2. Experimental 
2.1. Preparation of liquid polysulfide cathode 
Polysulfide catholyte, Li2S8, was prepared by directly reaction of 
stoichiometric amounts of sulfur powder (AR, ≥99.5%, Sigma-Aldrich) 
with lithium sulfide (99.5% trace metals basis, Sigma-Aldrich) in 1 M 
lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI)/DOL/DME /1 wt% 
LiNO3 solution with continuous magnetic stirring for 12 h in the argon- 
filled glove box (oxygen and moisture level below than 0.1 ppm) [6,25]. 
The preparation of solid S-based composite cathode is following the 
method published in a previous report [38]. 
2.2. Liquid lithium amalgam anode 
First, lithium metal foils (≥99.9%, Sigma-Aldrich) were cut into 
small pieces, and 0.858 wt% cut metal pieces added into 99.142 wt% 
mercury (≥99.999%, Sigma-Aldrich) in the argon-filled glove box (ox­
ygen and moisture level below than 0.1 ppm). The Li-Hg phase diagram 
was shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information). Then, the obtained 
mixture of lithium and mercury were left to stand in the container for 48 
h to form a liquid lithium amalgam at room temperature. To prepare the 
liquid lithium-based anode, the substrate (carbon cloth or Ni foam) was 
firstly cut to an area of 1.767 cm2, fully infiltrated by the obtained liquid 
lithium amalgam, and pressed in the die with 15 mm in diameter under

2.3. Materials characterization 
The morphology and element distribution of samples were examined 
by a field-emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM, Hitachi Co., 
SU8220) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM, Hitachi Co., SU8220), 
and Transmission electron microscope (TEM) analysis were tested on an 
FEI Co., Talos F200S. X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS, Thermo 
Fisher Co., Escalab 250Xi) was collected using 15 kV monochromatic Al 
Kα X-ray source (1361 eV) in hybrid lens mode with a step size of 1 eV 
and pass energy of 100 eV. X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis was per­
formed over the range of 10–90◦with a scanning rate of 20◦per min on a 
Bruker D8 Advance. Raman analysis was detected by Qontor (Renishaw, 
UK) with 532 nm laser. 
2.4. Electrochemical measurements 
A CR2032 coin cell was used to evaluate the performance of solid- 
state lithium-sulfur battery with liquid-solid interfaces. To fabricate 
the Li-S full cell, the pressed liquid lithium amalgam/substrate (carbon 
cloth or Ni foam) anode (0.94 V vs. Li/Li+) was used as mechanical 
support [39]. LGPS electrolyte power (80–120 mg) placed on the ob­
tained anode and pressed under 80 MPa for 2 min, and then Li2S8 so­
lution as cathode (0.5 M, 10 μl, a mass loading of sulfur = ~0.765 mg 
cm−2) were dropped on the solid-state electrolyte. Finally, the current 
collector was covered on the cathode. To prepare the current collector, 
an aluminium foil with a 200 μm blade-coater was coated by the mixture 
of 90 wt% graphene (Institute of Coal Chemistry, Chinese Academy of 
Science)/10 wt% polyvinylidenefluoride (PVDF)/N-methylpyrro-lidi­
none (NMP), dried at 60 ◦C for 12 h, and cut into disc. 
To further enhance the conductivity of the liquid cathode, a layer of 
the composite of LGPS@graphene powder was introduced on the surface 
of LGPS solid-state electrolyte, which was prepared by grinding the 
mixture of 70 wt% of LGPS solid-state electrolyte and 30 wt% graphene 
for 30 min. Similarly, the full cell of Li2S8|LGPS@graphene/LGPS|Li-Hg 
was fabricated by the above-mentioned method. The pressed anode was 
used as mechanical support, and LGPS electrolyte power (80–120 mg) 
were pressed on the obtained anode under 80 MPa for 2 min. Then 10 
mg LGPS@graphene composite powder were pressed on the anode- 
supported LGPS (80 MPa for 1 min). Li2S8 solution as cathode (0.17 
M, 30 μl, a mass loading of sulfur = ~0.765 mg cm−2) were dropped on 
the solid-state electrolyte. Finally, the current collector was covered on 
the cathode. 
For comparison, several other kinds of solid-state Li-S full cells were 
also fabricated. Similarly, after pressing the Li10GeP2S12 (LGPS, 99%, 
HF-Kejing) solid-state electrolyte powder and liquid lithium amalgam/ 
substrate (carbon cloth or Ni foam) anode, a limited liquid electrolyte 
was dropped on the surface of LGPS, and then 1 mg of solid S/graphene 
composite power placed on the wet LGPS for preparing the composite(S- 
graphene)/limited liquid electrolyte |LGPS| Li-Hg-Ni full cell. Without a 
limited liquid electrolyte, a solid composite (S-graphene-LGPS) |LGPS|
Li-Hg-Ni full cell was also fabricated. Using pure Li to replace the liquid 
anode, a liquid Li2S8|LGPS|pure Li was prepared. The liquid electrolyte 
was composed of 1 M Bis(trifluoromethane)-sulfonimide lithium salt 
(LiTFSI; Sigma Aldrich) in a 1:1 (by volume) mixture of 1, 2-dimethoxy­
ethane (DME; Sigma Aldrich) and 1, 3-dioxolane (DOL; Sigma Aldrich). 
For the fabrication of solid-state symmetric cells, both sides of a 
pressed LGPS solid-state electrolyte disc were sandwiched with two 
pieces of the liquid lithium amalgam/substrate (carbon cloth or Ni 
foam) anode at room temperature. First, solid-state electrolyte powder 
(80–120 mg) were pressed to form a disc, placed on the formed anode 
and pressed under 180–350 MPa for 2 min, and the other anode was 
placed onto the resulting disc with electrolytes and anode and pressed 
under 130–230 MPa for 2 min in the die to build a solid-state symmetric 
cell. All fabrication of cells was conducted in the argon-filled glove box. 
Schematic diagrams for the fabrication of solid-state lithium-sulfur 
battery with liquid-solid interfaces were shown in Fig. 1. 
The cells were galvanostatically discharged-charged by using a 
LAND battery tester (Wuhan LAND Co., China). The electrochemical 
impedance spectroscopy (EIS) measurements and cyclic voltammetry 
(CV) were conducted by a CHI660E electrochemical workstation. The 
frequency of EIS was ranged from 1 to 100 MHz with AC amplitude of 5 
mV. After testing, the impedance spectra were fitted by using Zview 
software. The sweep rate of CV was 0.1~0.5 mV s −1. 
3. Results and discussion 
To prepare disk anodes for coin-cell tests (Fig. 1), the liquid Li-Hg 
alloy is dropped and pressed into a disc substrate (carbon cloth or Ni 
foam) at room temperature (Fig. 1a, d, e). An alloy with 0.858 wt% of Li 
is chosen to ensure a wide range of liquid phase during changing and 
discharging process. Besides, the Li amount of liquid alloy anode can be 
precisely adjusted and it has an excellent interfacial contact with inor­
ganic solid-state electrolytes [40]. A thin LGPS electrolyte layer is 
capable of being well integrated in the resulting liquid anode after 
pressing (Fig. 1f, g). In the cathode side, precise amount of liquid Li2S8 
solution is dropped on the surface of solid–state electrolyte, and a small 
contact angle observed, showing that the liquid cathode has a good 
wettability with the solid-state LGPS electrolyte (Fig. 1b, j). As a result, a 
limited lithium anode supported solid-state Li-S batteries with 
liquid-solid anode/cathode-electrolyte interfaces can be fabricated 
(Fig. 1c, k). No liquid leakage occurs during the whole fabrication pro­
cess (Fig. 1f, g, h, i, l and m), indicating that the liquid electrodes have a 
good adhesion with solid-state electrolyte and substrate. 
Since the interface between pure Li metal and SSE is a solid-solid 
interface, and Li metal can react with LGPS electrolyte to easily cause 
a short circuit during cycling, the resulting cells show the poor interfa­
cial stability and large electrode/electrolyte interface impedance 
(Fig. 2a-c). To improve the interfacial compatibility and stability be­
tween anode and SSE, a liquid Li-amalgam anode is employed (Fig. 1d, 
e). As shown in Fig. 2a, the interface between liquid Li amalgam anode 
and LGPS in solid-state symmetric cells is extremely stable during 
cycling, exhibiting regular voltage fluctuations with small hysteresis and 
a long cycle life for 1000 h at 2.83 mA cm−2. No internal short circuit 
occurs as the current density increases from 0.28 to 56.59 mA cm−2 
(Fig. 2d-f and Figure S2a-c). Furthermore, the liquid Li based symmetric 
cells with solid-state electrolyte show much smaller interfacial imped­
ance (14 Ω) than the pure Li foil-based cell (6755 Ω) (Fig. 2b, c), owing 
to an enhanced interfacial contact between anode and electrolyte. 
To test the cycling performance of liquid Li-amalgam anodes at a 
high current density, the symmetric cells were operated at the ultra-high 
current density of 56.59 mA cm−2 for 10,000 cycles at room temperature 
(Fig. 2g and Figure S2d). As expected, the Li-Hg/LGPS symmetrical cell 
affords the excellent cycle stability without obvious polarization and Li 
dendrite formation under ultra-high current density of 56.59 mA cm−2. 
Flat and stable stripping/plating plateaus are maintained after 10,000 
cycles. The decrease of overpotential in the first few cycles may be due to 
the surface activation and generation of new interface during repeated 
Li+ plating/stripping process [41–42]. The excellent stability and small 
polarization potential of room temperature liquid alloy anode-based 
symmetric cells can be attributed to their high chemical stability, 
excellent physical contact, and high ionic conductivity (Fig. 2b, c). 
Therefore, the liquid limited Li anode greatly helps reducing the inter­
face impedance and improving interfacial stability between anode and 
LGPS SSE, especially at high current density.

shown in Fig. 3, liquid Li2S8|LGPS|Li-Hg cell (a mass loading of sulfur ≈
0.765 mg cm−2) shows a better cycling performance and lower inter­
facial impedance than any full cells in our tests, such as solid composite

However, since the liquid Li2S8 cathode has an intimate contact and a

affect a solid-liquid phase transition of interface between cathode and 
electrolyte during cycling.

discharge curves and cyclic voltammetry curves (Fig. 3), it could be 
confirmed that during the first discharge, liquid Li2S8 cathode could be 
converted into Li2S2 and Li2S through the following two-step reactions 
[38]:  
Li2S8 → Li2S4–6 (1.269 V)                                                                       
Li2S4–6 → Li2S / Li2S2 (1.057 V)                                                             
And during charging, Li2S2 and Li2S could be converted into Li2S4–8 
and S through the following one-step reaction:  
Li2S / Li2S2 → Li2S8 / S (1.542 V)

based solid-state lithium-sulfur battery in our system, is similar to that of 
the traditional lithium sulfur battery with liquid electrolyte [4–6]., and 
different from that of all-solid-state Li-S battery [13–17].

4. Conclusion 
We successfully design and demonstrate a liquid cathode/anode 
based, room-temperature, solid-state lithium sulfur battery with low- 
impedance electrode/electrolyte interfaces. In this energy storage sys­
tem, the liquid Li-amalgam anode with Ni foam or carbon cloth is 
capable of eliminating lithium dendrite, has a good contact with solid- 
state Li10GeP2S12 (LGPS) electrolyte, and can be employed as mechan­
ical support to fabricate solid-state batteries. The limited lithium anode 
based symmetric batteries with solid-state electrolyte show an excellent 
electrochemical performance, and can safely and smoothly run at 55.6 
mA cm−2 for 10,000 cycles. Solid electrolyte can alleviate the shuttle 
effect of polysulfides, prevent direct contact of lithium amalgam with 
polysulfide, and solve the issues of electrolyte leakage and battery 
safety. Li2S8 polysulfide catholyte is able to offer a high active material 
loading (≥135.2 mg mL−1), prevent the issues of volume expansion and 
poor kinetics of Li2S, and reduce the interface impedance of cathode/ 
electrolyte. As a result, this strategy provides a pathway to resolve some 
of the fundamental interfacial and electrode issues of solid-state Li-S 
batteries.
