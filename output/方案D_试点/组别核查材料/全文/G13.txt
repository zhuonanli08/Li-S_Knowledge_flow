A new high-capacity cathode for all-solid-state lithium sulfur battery 
Qing Wang a,b, Ya Chen a, Jun Jin a, Zhaoyin Wen a,*

A B S T R A C T  
A new composite sulfur cathode with high sulfur loading for all-solid-state lithium sulfur (Li–S) battery, along 
with an in-situ coating process for preparing this composite cathode, is proposed in this manuscript. This 
composite cathode includes carbonized cotton fibers as the electron conductive skeleton, sulfide electrolyte 
coating on the carbon fibers as the Li-ion conductor, and elemental sulfur as the active material. The in-situ 
coating of sulfide electrolyte is carried out via a liquid-phase process, which results in a close contact be­
tween carbon fiber and the sulfide electrolyte. The subsequent mixing and heating treatment with elemental 
sulfur form a composite cathode with good electrochemical performance. In the assembled all-solid-state Li–S 
battery with Li6PS5Cl electrolyte and lithium metal anode, the composite cathode delivers a super high discharge 
capacity of 32.8 mAh.   
1. Introduction 
Conventional lithium ion battery is reaching its limits in terms of 
safety and durability, posing a challenge for application in large scale 
electrical equipment [1,2]. A shift to rechargeable lithium sulfur (Li–S) 
battery with much higher theoretical capacity (1675 mAh g−1) and 
energy density (2600 Wh kg−1) is a promising alternative to improve 
safety, energy density, and durability. The natural abundance and 
environmental friendliness of elemental sulfur make Li–S batteries 
more likely to industrialize on a large scale [3–5]. Despite the attractive 
promise, the development of Li–S batteries based on organic liquid 
electrolyte still falls short of expectation largely due to the shuttle of 
highly soluble polysulfides in liquid electrolyte and the growth of 
lithium dendrites [6–8]. Moreover, in consideration of the electronically 
and ionically insulating nature of active material sulfur, a large amount 
of conducting additives such as carbon materials is necessary in the 
composite cathode, thus significantly reducing the practical capacity. To 
solve the problem of shuttle effect, using solid electrolytes such as oxide 
ceramic electrolytes or sulfide glass-ceramic electrolytes in Li–S bat­
teries appear to be a workable way [9,10]. However, the huge imped­
ance caused by the solid-solid contact among particles in cathode, along 
with the interfacial impedance between electrodes and solid electro­
lytes, is still a big barrier to the application of this type of batteries [11]. 
Although adding some liquid electrolyte into the cathode can solve the 
impedance problems when using oxide ceramic electrolytes, we still 
cannot avoid the dissolution of the polysulfides into the liquid electro­
lyte, causing capacity fading [12]. In our previous research, a little 
amount of solvent-in-salt (SIS) or fluorinated liquid electrolyte (FDE) 
was used to wet the cathode/ceramic interface, resulting in very good 
cycling stability, but after all, this method cannot completely avoid 
safety issues [13,14]. 
Sulfide electrolyte materials like Li2S-P2S5 and its derivatives possess 
high lithium ion conductivity and natural softness, they can be densified 
easily by cold processing and blended into S cathode of solid-state Li–S 
batteries by ball-milling together with conductive carbon [15–18]. In 
this case, the isolated granular carbon material will not form conductive 
network within the composite cathode. Furthermore, it is crucial to 
ensure that the active material sulfur has a close contact with the ion 
conductor and the electron conductor. Here, a feasible solution is to 
construct such a composite, in which carbon fiber works as the 
conductive skeleton, and sulfide electrolyte is coated on the surface of 
the carbon fiber via liquid-phase process. After mixing with elemental 
sulfur and heating process, the composite sulfur cathode with close inner 
contact is obtained. Thus, the carbon fiber functions to conduct electrons 
rapidly, and the sulfide electrolyte coated thereon can rapidly conduct 
lithium ions, and then the active substance sulfur forms good contact 
with the electron conductor and the ion conductor both. 
In this work, cotton fiber was chosen as the precursor of carbon fiber 
conducting electron in the composite cathode. The first step is synthesis 
of dispersed carbon fiber from cotton, in which concentrated sulfuric

2. Experimental section 
2.1. Synthesis of dispersed cotton carbon fiber 
Medical absorbent cotton (3 g) was immersed into 40 mL of deion­
ized water dissolved with 20 mL of concentrated sulfuric acid (c-H2SO4) 
and stirred for 10 min. The obtained slurry-like mixture was filtered, 
thoroughly washed with deionized water and ethyl alcohol, and then 
vacuum-dried overnight at 70 ◦C. The dried cotton fiber is placed in a 
quartz crucible, and heated to 1000 ◦C at a rate of 5 ◦C min−1 in an Ar 
atmosphere and held for 2 h, then naturally cooled to room temperature 
to obtain the carbonized dispersed cotton fiber (CDCF). 
2.2. In-situ coating of Li7P3S11 sulfide electrolyte 
Considering the extreme sensitivity of sulfide electrolyte to moisture, 
all the preparation processes were performed in an Ar-filled glovebox. 
The Li7P3S11 electrolyte was synthesized from Li2S (Alfa Aesar, 99.9%) 
and P2S5 (Sigma-Aldrich, 99%) in acetonitrile (Selectilyte BASF, battery 
grade). Firstly, 0.1 g of CDCF powder was added into acetonitrile (ACN) 
solvent (5 mL) and stirred thoroughly. Then Li2S and P2S5 were dis­
solved into the solution with molar ratio of 7:3 and stirred for 24 h at 
70 ◦C. The total mass of Li2S and P2S5 here is about 0.2 g. Containing 
gray precipitates formed during the mixing process, the solution was 
evaporated in vacuum at 50 ◦C in avoid of liquid boiling. The deposited 
precursor powder was further heated under vacuum at 80 ◦C for 6 h to 
remove the residual solvents completely. After drying, the powder was 
heated at 225 ◦C in Ar atmosphere in order to crystallize into the 
Li7P3S11 phase (the reason for choosing 225 ◦C is in Supplementary #1 
in the Supporting Information). The final composite powder is herein­
after referred to as CDCF@Li7P3S11. 
2.3. Pure Li7P3S11 sulfide electrolyte preparation 
Pure Li7P3S11 electrolyte was synthesized from Li2S and P2S5 in 
acetonitrile (ACN) solvent. 0.06432 g of Li2S and 0.1334 g of P2S5 were 
dissolved into 5 mL of CAN and then stirred for 24 h at 70 ◦C. Subse­
quently, the solution containing white precipitate was evaporated in 
vacuum at 50 ◦C in avoid of liquid boiling. The deposited precursor 
powder was further heated under vacuum at 80 ◦C for 6 h to remove the 
residual solvents completely. After drying, the powder was heated at 
225 ◦C in Ar atmosphere in order to crystallize into the Li7P3S11 phase. 
Thus, we obtained the pure Li7P3S11 electrolyte. 
2.4. Composite sulfur cathode preparation 
In order to mix the active material sulfur powder and 
CDCF@Li7P3S11 thoroughly with a weight ratio of 1:1, the mixture was 
ground by hand-grinding in an agate mortar for 1 h. Then the sieved 
composite was sealed in crucible and heated at 155 ◦C for 12 h under 
vacuum, forming CDCF@S@Li7P3S11. 
2.5. Li6PS5Cl electrolyte synthesis 
Dry LiCl, P2S5 and Li2S were weighed with molar ratio of 2:1:5. 
Firstly, LiCl and P2S5 particles were thoroughly ground and mixed uni­
formly. Then, Li2S was added and ground for 15 min to ensure a suffi­
cient mixing of the three raw materials. Thereafter, the powder mixture 
was heat-treated at 550 ◦C for 6 h in an inert atmosphere at a heating 
rate of 5 ◦C min−1. After naturally cooled to room temperature, the 
obtained agglomerate was pulverized and ground to obtain a Li6PS5Cl 
powder. 
2.6. Full battery assembly 
The working electrode powder CDCF@S@Li7P3S11 (0.1 g) and solid 
electrolyte powder Li6PS5Cl (0.2 g) were placed in a stainless steel 
mould (14 mm diameter) and pressed together under 100 MPa at room 
temperature. The lithium metal foil was placed on the surface of the 
solid electrolyte side of the bilayer pellet, and pressed with 100 MPa 
pressure again to obtain a tri-layered pellet. The tri-layered pellet was 
finally sandwiched with two stainless-steel disks as current collectors. 
All the cell preparation processes were conducted in a dry Ar-filled 
glovebox. 
2.7. Characterization 
SEM images were obtained by scanning electron microscope (SEM, 
Hitachi JSM 6700). Raman spectroscopy was done using Thermo Sci­
entific DXR in order to verify the phosphorus-sulfide anion structure in 
the obtained samples. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) 
was carried out on an Autolab electrochemical workstation (ECO 
CHEMIE B⋅V, Netherlands) with the Frequency Response Analyzer. The 
charge and discharge curves for the solid-state lithium sulfur batteries 
were measured with LAND CT2001A battery test system (China) in 
voltage range from 3 V to 1.5 V under current density of 0.05 mA at 
60 ◦C. 
3. Results and discussion 
To tackle the large interfacial resistance in the sulfur cathode of the 
all-solid-state Li–S battery, a general strategy is to mix the elemental 
sulfur with sulfide electrolyte and conductive carbon materials by ball 
milling [19,20]. Nevertheless, the internal contact in this composite 
cathode obtained by above traditional methods still falls short of 
expectation largely. As shown in Fig. 1, a viable solution is to use 
conductive carbon fibers as the conductive skeleton of the composite 
cathode, in which an in-situ liquid-phase method and a melt diffusion 
process guarantee a close contact among the sulfur, sulfide electrolyte 
and the carbon fibers. In this case, the skeleton of the cotton carbon fi­
bers plays a role in quickly conducting electrons, and the sulfide elec­
trolyte Li7P3S11 coated thereon can quickly conduct lithium ions. After 
mixing and heating with elemental sulfur, the composite cathode was 
obtained finally. 
Medical absorbent cotton was adopted as the natural carbon source 
in the work. If directly carbonized at 1000 ◦C for 2 h in Ar atmosphere 
without any hydrolysis pretreatment, the cotton formed a mass of cross- 
linked carbon fiber (Fig. 2a), which is not suitable for subsequent 
coating process. According to the SEM images of the directly carbonized 
cotton fibers shown in Fig. 2b and Fig. 2c, in addition to a hollow 
structure, the cotton carbon fibers has so many micro-wrinkles on the 
surface caused by the volume shrinkage during the carbonization pro­
cess, which is beneficial for subsequent adhesion of sulfide electrolyte 
particles during the liquid coating process.

certain degree of graphitized carbon appears in the cotton carbon fiber

above results, we are confident that the composite CDCF@Li7P3S11 has 
both good electronic conductivity and outstanding ionic conductivity. 
A subsequent melt diffusion process turned the CDCF@Li7P3S11 into 
the composite sulfur cathode CDCF@S@Li7P3S11. To construct an all- 
solid-state Li–S battery with above cathode, it’s vital to adopt a sul­
fide electrolyte with super high ionic conductivity. 
Then the Li-argyrodite Li6PS5X (X = Cl, Br, and I) providing Li-ion 
conductivities of up to 10−3 S cm−1 at room temperature became the 
first choice [25,26]. The XRD pattern of the as-prepared Li6PS5Cl pow­
der is given in Fig. 4a, apart from some weak peaks of Li2S and LiCl, the 
overall diffraction pattern is well matched with that of Li7PS6 
(PDF#34–0688), indicating that the Li6PS5Cl powder with high purity 
has been successfully obtained by hand grinding and subsequent heat 
treatment [27]. The Li6PS5Cl powder was pressed into pellets with a 
pressure of 1 T, and then subjected to a heat treatment at 550 ◦C for 6 h. 
According to the EIS spectrum of the Li6PS5Cl pellet at room tempera­
ture (Fig. 4b), the calculated ionic conductivity is 5.24 × 10−3 S cm−1 
(the calculation process is in Supplementary #2 in the Supporting In­
formation), which is much higher than many reported results 
[25,26,28,29]. The EIS spectra of the Li6PS5Cl pellet at gradient tem­
peratures from −10 ◦C to 60 ◦C were shown in Fig. 4c, indicating that as 
the temperature increases, the total impedance decreases significantly. 
When the temperature exceeded 60 ◦C, the ionic conductivity reached 
the order of 10−2 S cm−1. Fig. 4d is the Arrhenius plot of the Li6PS5Cl 
pellet, the activation energy is calculated to be 0.277 eV (the calculation 
process is in Supplementary #2 in the Supporting Information).

4. Conclusion 
In conclusion, a new composite cathode, containing elemental sulfur, 
carbonized cotton fibers and Li7P3S11 sulfide electrolyte coated on the 
fibers’ surface, is proposed for all-solid-state batteries. In this work, 
concentrated sulfuric acid was adopted to hydrolyze the medical 
absorbent cotton into single fibers. After carbonization, the obtained 
carbonized dispersed cotton fibers were coated by sulfide electrolyte via 
a liquid-phase process and subsequent heat treatment. Followed by a 
melt diffusion process with elemental sulfur, an intimate contact was 
achieved among the sulfur, the carbon fiber and the ion conductor. 
Besides, Li6PS5Cl electrolyte with high ionic conductivity of 5.24 ×
10−3 S cm−1 at room temperature is prepared by solid phase reaction.
