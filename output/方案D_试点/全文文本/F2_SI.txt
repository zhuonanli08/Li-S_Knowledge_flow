1. Experimental methods 
Cathode preparation: Li6PS5Cl (SE) was synthesized through classic solid-state reaction by mixing the 
precursors (LiCl, Li2S, and P2S5) with a mortar and pestle, pelletizing the resulting mixture with a manual 
screw press, and annealing the pellet at 550 ℃ in a sealed ampule for a week.1,2 X-ray diffraction and 
temperature dependent impedance spectroscopy of the resulting sample showed phase purity (impurities 
less than 1 wt%), the ionic conductivity (1.8 mS cm-1), and the activation energy of the ionic conductivity 
(0.38 eV) as shown in previous work.3 Further details of the synthesis and the measurement procedures can 
be found elsewhere.1,2,4 The SE-carbon (C) composites with the mass ratios of m(SE):m(C) = 90:10, 80:20, 
and 70:30 were obtained by ball-milling the SE and the conductive additive (ECP600JD, LION). The 
milling was conducted with the total 200 mg of SE-C mixture with 30 g of ZrO2 milling media with the 
diameter of 5 mm at 600 rpm for four hours. A 10-minute break was taken every 10 minutes of milling. As 
a comparison, as-synthesized Li6PS5Cl was also ball-milled under the same condition without carbon 
additives, showing an ionic conductivity of (0.76 mS cm-1) with the activation energy of 0.39 eV. The 
cathode composite for the solid-state Li-S batteries, i. e. the SE-C-S composite, was produced by two-step 
milling. First, a sulfur-carbon (ECP600JD) mixture was prepared in the same way as in ref 2, followed by 
milling with Li6PS5Cl with a weight ratio of 1:1 (resulting mass ratio is m(SE):m(C):m(S) = 3:1:2). The 
milling processes were conducted with the same parameters as the ones for the SE-C composite. Due to the 
sensitivity to air and moisture, all the sample handlings were done under Ar protective atmosphere.

Step-wise cyclic voltammetry (CV): Step-wise CV is useful to address materials´ electrochemical stability 
and the redox behavior of the decomposition products.5 80 mg of Li6PS5Cl (102 mg cm-2) were loaded into 
the cell with a diameter of 10 mm (area of 0.785 cm2) as separator and prepressed with a manual press in 
the glove box, followed by loading 12 mg (15.3 mg cm-2) of SE-C composite prepared with the weight ratio 
of SE:C is 9:1. The cell stack was electronically pre-pressed under 380 MPa and then the In or In and Li 
foils were loaded on the other side of the separator as anode. All cells were fixed in a metal frame to 
maintain a pressure at around 50 MPa. Each scan was started from around the open circuit voltage to the 
chosen reversal potential (vs. In/InLi as reference potential) with a scan speed of 0.1 mV s-1, followed by 
the reverse scan with – 0.1 mV s-1. The following scan was then conducted to the next reversal potential, 
which was higher or lower by 0.1 V for the oxidative and reductive runs, respectively. The oxidative or 
reductive step-wise CVs were performed up to 3.5 V or down to 0 V vs. In/InLi potential. In foil was 
employed as anode material for the oxidative step-wise CV.5 One important note on the usage of In foil is 
that, although the reference potential is not fixed before the measurement, it is pinned to 0.62 V vs. Li+/Li 
(In/InLi reference potential) as soon as the measurement started with a negligible amount of current flow.6,7 
In and Li foils were employed to supply a sufficient amount of lithium to test the reduction stability through 
reductive step-wise CV. The employed In and Li foils possess the diameter of 9 mm and 6 mm and thickness 
of 100 μm and 120 μm, respectively. The resulting molar ratio is mol(In):mol(Li) = 3:2 to ensure the In/InLi 
anode potential of 0.62 V vs. Li+/Li as a reference potential.7 Details of the oxidative step-wise CV with 
hand-ground SE-C composites can be found in ref 5. All the CV tests were conducted at 25 ℃. 
Cycling tests: The reversibility of the redox reactions of degradation products of a SE-C composite were 
investigated by cycling the SE-C composite like a cathode material. Here, the mass ratio m(SE):m(C) = 9:1 
was employed. The cells were cycled within 1 − 1.8 V, 1 − 2.4 V, 1 − 2.7 V, and 1 − 3.0 V vs. In/InLi, as 
well as 0 − 2.7 V vs. In/InLi with a constant current of 446 μA (568 μA cm-2). The total loading of the SE-
C composite was 12 mg (15.3 mg cm-2), corresponding to the SE loading of 10.8 mg (13.8 mg cm-2). The 
cycling tests with a SE-C-S composite (the mass ratio m(SE):m(C):m(S) = 3:1:2) were performed with a 
total cathode loading of 8 mg (10.2 mg cm-2), corresponding to a sulfur loading of 2.67 mg (3.40 mg cm-2). 
In both cases, cells were constructed with 80 mg of as-synthesized SE separator (102 mg cm-2) with In and 
Li foils, possessing the diameter of 9 mm and 6 mm and the thickness of 100 μm and 120 μm, respectively. 
The applied current for cycling was 446 μA (568 μA cn-2), corresponding to 0.1 C. The lower and upper 
cutoff potentials were 0 or 1 V and 2.7 or 3.0 V. All cycling tests were performed inside the climate chamber 
at 25 ℃. 
Impedance spectroscopy: Impedance spectroscopy was performed to analyze the Li ion and electron 
transport in the composite cathodes with a VMP-300 impedance analyzer (BioLogic). The resulting

impedance spectra were analyzed by fitting the impedance spectra with a transmission-line model (TLM) 
using RelaxIS 3 (rhd instruments, Germany). The perturbation amplitude was set to 10 mV after confirming 
no apparent change in spectra with 5 mV and 20 mV. The applied frequency ranges are varied depending 
on the time-constant of the cells (within the range of 7 MHz – 5 mHz). All cells were measured in metal 
frames to maintain the pressure at around 50 MPa. Thickness dependency test: To validate a TLM analysis, 
three cell configurations with a m(SE):m(C) = 90:10 composite were prepared as shown in Figure 3. Cell(i) 
was a cathode half-cell with 15.3 mg cm-2 of SE-C composite with an anode with In/InLi potential prepared 
with In and Li foils as mentioned above (102 mg cm-2 of SE as a separator). The anode foils were loaded 
after pre-pressing the cathode and separator under 380 MPa for 3 min. Cell(ii) and Cell(iii) were symmetric 
cells with either 15.3 or 30.6 mg cm-2 of SE-C composite on both sides of the cells (102 mg cm-2 of SE as 
a separator). The entire cells were pressed with 380 MPa for 3 min. The equilibration time was at least 3 
hours and the measurements were repeated for 2 times to double-check reversibility. To remove the noise 
in the low-frequency range, all impedance measurements were performed in a climate chamber with the 
temperature controlled to be 25 ℃. Temperature dependent tests: The temperature dependent 
measurements were performed between – 40 ℃ to 60 ℃, with an hour of equilibration time except the first 
step from room temperature to – 40 ℃ where the equilibration time was two hours. The same procedure 
was applied in the temperature dependent impedance measurement to evaluate Ea in the ball-milled SE. To 
evaluate the activation energy of ionic transport in the composites, only the data taken below 25 ℃ were 
included to minimize the influence of electronic transport. This is to secure the required condition of rion ≫ 
re (see Section 2.2.3). Potential dependent tests: To test the potential dependence, two identical cells with 
the cell (i) configuration in Figure 3 were constructed. One was used for the reduction test and the other 
one was used for the oxidation test. In both cases, the potential of the working electrode is scanned from 
OCV (about 1.55 V vs. In/InLi) to the target potential with 0.1 mV s-1 and kept at the target potential for 12 
h to let the degradation proceed. The charge consumed or delivered while scanning and holding the potential 
are also monitored to assess the degree of degradation. After an hour of cell relaxation, the impedance 
measurement was conducted, followed by the next potential scan to the target potential lower or higher by 
0.1 V. The measurements are performed down to 0 V for the reductive test and up to 3 V for the oxidative 
test. While a TLM analysis could be applied all the way down to 0 V vs. In/InLi for the reduction test, it 
was applicable only up to 2.4 vs. In/InLi for the oxidation test. The total resistance of the cathode layer 
above 2.5 V vs. In/InLi was well fitted by a semi-circle, reflecting a highly resistive interphase being the 
rate-determining process.

Analytical investigations: The phase purity of the employed Li6PS5Cl before processing was confirmed 
by X-ray diffraction (XRD) before processing.2 The thickness of the SE-C composite layers was determined 
by cross-section images taken by a Zeiss MERLIN scanning electron microscope (Carl Zeiss). A transfer 
vessel (Leica EM VC500) was used to transfer the disassembled battery cells, which were snapped to 
expose the cross sections. Measurements were carried out by application of an acceleration voltage of 5 kV 
and a probing current of 1000 pA with backscattered electrons. The results are shown in Figure S5. The 
binding energy of the SE in cathode composites before and after cycling, as well as before and after mixing 
with carbon additives were investigated with X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). All the sample 
handlings were conducted under protective argon atmosphere, and the sample transfer was performed with 
the transfer module to eliminate the exposure to air and moisture. To rule out the shift in the energy from 
of surface effects, a very gentle sputtering was applied before obtaining the profiles for each element (Ar+ 
sputtering with 0.5 kV and 0.5 µA on the area of 36 mm² for 5 min). The analysis was conducted with Casa 
XPS software. All spectra were charge corrected to a binding energy of 284.8 eV for the C 1s line. The 
peak separation of the doublets found in S 2p and P 2p spectra were 1.21 and 0.87 eV, respectively. The 
full-width-half-maxima (FWHM) of all the doublets were constrained to evaluate the peak broadening as 
an indication of a glass formation. The higher the FWHM is, possibly the higher the amorphous contents 
are due to the variation in the binding energy. All the results are in Figure S2.

4. DC polarization measurements 
The DC polarization measurements were performed to corroborate the effective ionic and electronic 
conductivities obtained through impedance measurements and the TLM analysis. Figure S3 shows the 
schematics of the measurement setups with electron-blocking (In/InLi|SE|SE-C|SE|In/InLi) and ion-
blocking (stainless-steel|SE-C|stainless-steel) electrodes, as well as their measurement results. The range of 
the potential applied to the working electrode is 5 ≤ |V| ≤ 50 mV. Each potential has been kept for 12 h to 
fully polarize before the steady direct current was registered. To omit the influence of floating potentials, 
the current direction is flipped for each potential step as indicated with the negative voltages in Figure b 
and d. As indicated in the figures, the resistances arising from the ionic and electronic transport can be well 
extracted. To obtain the effective ionic conductivity of the SE-C composite Rion from the measured total 
resistance Rion,DC, it is necessary to subtract additional contributions, such as the contribution from the 
electron blocking layers (orange SE layers, RSE) and the contribution from the interface between SE layer 
and InLi anode, RSE/InLi. Each RSE can be estimated to be 36 Ω as there is 60 mg of the SE layer with 1.8 mS 
cm-1 (See Figure SX as well, as the resistance is 60 Ω with 100 mg of SE). The 2RSE/InLi is determined to be 
26.6 Ω based on the In/InLi-SE-In/InLi symmetric cell result shown in Figure S4. With the information of 
the cell constant, the effective ionic conductivity of the composite is calculated to be 0.17 mS cm-1. The 
ionic conductivity measured by DC (0.17 mS cm-1) is slightly lower than that by AC shown in Figure 4c in 
the main text (0.20 ± 0.02 mS cm-1). This is possibly due to the interface that is not captured here (e.g. 
interface between SE and SE-C layers), but the difference is very subtle and negligible. As for the electronic 
transport, the measured resistance (Re,DC) can directly be converted to the effective electronic conductivity, 
resulting in the value of 1.9 mS cm-1 which is in excellent agreement with AC result (1.9 ± 0.2 mS cm-1). 
As explained in Figure S3, considering the fact that a widely-used semi-circle fitting on the lower frequency 
tail overestimates the effective ionic conductivity by almost a factor of four, the results above clearly 
highlight the value of the appropriate impedance analysis with a TLM.

The thicknesses of the composite layers are evaluated with the cross-section images obtained from scanning 
electron microscopy with back-scattered electrons as shown in Figure S5. Although the interface shows 
some roughness as seen in the figure, it is safe to assume that the thickness of the cathode composites takes

in the TLM analysis. It should be noted that, while it is not easy to exactly quantify, the roughness of the 
interface may contribute to the variation in the resulting effective conductivities; however, these errors are 
estimated to be lower than the observed differences in the various measurements presented in this work.

9. Comparison of the fitting results obtained with a simple RC parallel circuit vs. those obtained 
with a TLM 
As stated in the main text, the wide-spread fit model with a simple semi-circle can lead to a severe 
underestimation of the cell resistance, resulting in overestimated effective conductivities. This can be 
critical in elucidating the correct failure mechanism of the cell of interest. Here, we demonstrate the fitting 
of the lower frequency range of the data from cell (iii) with an equivalent circuit of Roff-RLF/CPELF-CPE, 
where RLF/CPELF is a parallel circuit of the resistance and a constant phase element of the low-frequency 
part (RLF and CPELF), respectively. Although the fitting result shows a decent quality of the fit (Figure S7), 
the resulting effective ionic conductivity is almost four times higher than that from a proper TLM analysis 
as in Table S4. The resistance contribution from the LF part is 64.1 Ω, which is much lower than 217 Ω 
obtained from a TLM analysis, resulting in the calculated effective ionic conductivity of 0.72 mS cm-1. This 
value is obviously almost four times higher than that obtained from a TLM analysis, and also the results 
from DC measurement. Furthermore, the resistance contribution from the offset (Roff) can be overestimated 
since the electronic contribution is included there when the electronic resistance in the composite is not 
negligible. Therefore, there are multifold reasons that a TLM analysis is necessary for the correct analysis 
of the well-mixed composite cathode like in the case of this work. 
 
Figure S7: An exemplary fit with a simple semi-circle with a parallel circuit of a resistance and a constant 
phase element. Although the resulting fit is apparently not far off from the data with a deformed semi-circle, 
the obtained effective ionic transport is severely overestimated.

15. Fit results of the potential dependence tests 
The dependence on the applied potentials is investigated by constructing two identical cells with SE-C 
composite with a mass ratio of m(SE):m(C) = 9:1 (9010 SE-C composite). The loading of the composite 
was 15.3 mg cm-2. Upon applying the potential, a Faradaic reaction occurs and charge flows. The observed 
charge at each potential is referred to as a specific charge in the table. The cumulative charge means the 
sum of all the charges down/up to a given potential. The representative impedance data from the oxidative 
and reductive tests in Nyquist plots are shown in Figure S11. Contributions to the total resistance from each 
process are also tabulated in Table S10. One thing to note is that the SE-C composite utilized for this test 
is prepared in the same way as for the other tests but comes from a different batch, resulting in a slightly 
higher effective ionic transport (by a factor of two) in the composite. While this batch dependent variation 
in the ion transport is apparent, we consider this as not significant compared to the change associated with 
a varied SE fraction. Since the effective ionic conductivities of both cells for reductive and oxidative tests 
are basically identical, we conclude this variation is not due to the impedance measurement or its analysis. 
We also need to note that, while we assign the small resistive contribution at around the same time constant 
as the origin of the part relevant for TLM analysis to the interface between anode and SE, this process at 
low potential can possibly be due to the electronic or ionic conduction in the decomposition products at low 
potential. The exact assignment of such process is not straightforward and the spectroscopic and 
electrochemical methods employed in this work cannot determine it reliably. As for the electronic transport 
in the composite, it can be resolved reliably only in limited cases since its resistance contribution is much 
lower than that of ionic transport, especially after the electrochemical treatment. In the oxidation tests, a 
TLM analysis is applicable only up to 2.4 V because of an emerging process and the total resistances above 
2.4 V are determined by fitting the spectra with a simple semi-circle.

16. Potential profiles of the Li-S batteries with various cutoff potentials 
 
Figure S12: Potential profiles of 1st, 2nd, 5th, 10th, 20th, 30th, 40th, and 50th cycles as well as the initial 
discharge of the cells cycled within 1 – 2.7 V and 1 – 3.0 V. It is evident that the cell cycled up to 3 V

potential profiles. A larger overpotential results in a less reversible capacity upon long-term cycling, 
indicating the progressively slowed down lithium ion kinetics caused by the oxidative SE degradation. The 
second charge curve shows a lower capacity due to the interruption during the discharge followed by the 
initial discharge. The reproducibility of these two cells are shown in Figure S14.
