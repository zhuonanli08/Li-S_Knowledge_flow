=== 第 14 页 ===
 
 
Supplementary Figure 12. PBE-DFT calculated DOS for pristine LPSCl1.5 (a), 
graphene-LPSCl1.5 (b) and two configurations of C3N4-LPSCl1.5 (c & d) composite, 
respectively. 
 
We used PBE-DFT calculations to calculate total and atom-projected electronic density 
of states (DOS) for LPSCl1.5, graphene-LPSCl1.5, and C3N4-LPSCl1.5 to understand and 
compare the electronic transport at the interfaces. While PBE-DFT tends to 
underestimate band gaps, we focused on comparison of atom-projected and total DOS 
at various interfaces. Specifically, LPSCl1.5 DOS shows a localized DOS near the Fermi 
level which arises from the partial DOS of S2-. The interaction of LPSCl1.5 and C 
delocalizes these S2- partial DOS as observed in graphene-LPSCl1.5 DOS plots. On the 
other hand, LPSCl1.5 interaction with C3N4 does not affect the localized states of S2- 
which implies its less favorable oxidation in C3N4-LPSCl1.5 composites compared to 
graphene-LPSCl1.5. 
 
 

=== 第 15 页 ===
 
Supplementary Figure 13. The DFT computed electronic DOS of graphene (a), N-
doped graphene (3% N, b), C3N4 (c), and CNG (d). For simplicity we used graphitic N 
for the nitrogen doped graphene structural model. 
 
The calculated DOS show that carbon exhibits conducting behaviour with a 0 eV band 
gap, and doping it with a small amount of nitrogen (~3%) still keep its conductive nature. 
In contrast, C3N4 displays broad bands near the valence band maximum and conduction 
band minimum, but retains a wide band gap (1.54 eV), indicating its semiconducting 
behaviour and poor conductivity compared to carbon and nitrogen-doped carbon. 
However, CNG exhibit a closing of the band gap in C3N4, and a small DOS emerges 
near the Fermi energy, primarily governed by the carbon of the nitrogen-doped layer. 
This interaction should give rise to enhancement in the conductivity of the overall 
structure, making the resultant material more conductive than C3N4 alone.

Supplementary note 2 
 
Coulombic efficiency ΦQ represents the ratio of charge released during discharge to the 
charge necessary for charging the battery in the nth cycle, ΦQ = Qdis, n/Qch, n where Qdis, 
n and Qch, n are the capacity of discharge and charge at the nth cycle, respectively. For 
sulfur cathodes, the first discharge is defined as an initial discharge, followed by cycles 
consisting of a charge followed by a discharge. Conversion efficiency ΦC is also used 
to describe the amount of recovered capacity upon charge over the previous discharge 
process, ΦC = Qch, n/Qdis, (n-1).  
Briefly, coulombic efficiency evaluates the recoverable capability of S back to Li2S, 
while conversion efficiency evaluates the recoverable capability of Li2S back to S.
